A. From IRD DIPNs

Illustration ??Industrial building sale: buyer/seller allowance split DIPN 2

A factory is built for $500,000 in February 1992. Initial allowance 20% = $100,000; annual allowance 4% = $20,000 in the first year, continuing for 3 more years, leaving a residue of expenditure of $320,000. The building is then sold for $420,000. The seller faces a balancing charge of $100,000 (sale price exceeds residue by $100,000, capped at allowances previously granted). The buyer's future annual allowance is recalculated as 1/21 (years remaining to the 25th year) ? $420,000 = $20,000/year.

Illustration ??Plant & machinery pooling across two rate classes DIPN 7

A taxpayer buys a motor car for $150,000 and 3 cookers for $50,000 (both 30% pool), plus office furniture for $20,000 and air-conditioning for $28,000 (both 20% pool). Year 1: 30% pool ??cost $200,000, initial allowance 60% = $120,000, annual allowance on the remaining $80,000 at 30% = $24,000. 20% pool ??similar mechanics give a smaller annual allowance. Total first-year depreciation allowance across both pools: $176,640.

Illustration ??Balancing charge capped at allowances previously granted DIPN 7

On cessation of a business, a 30% pool with a reducing value brought forward of $21,000 (original cost $75,000) is sold for $80,000. Although sale proceeds ($80,000) exceed the reducing value ($21,000) by $59,000, the balancing charge is capped at $54,000 ??the total allowances previously granted ($75,000 cost ??$21,000 residue) ??never more than was originally allowed.

Illustration ??Accelerating pre-2018/19 environmental instalments to 100% DIPN 5

Company-HK pays $15M, $20M and $30M in May 2016/2017/2018 respectively for environmental protection installations, under the pre-2018/19 5-instalment (20%/year) regime. Electing under s.16I(3A) to accelerate all remaining instalments to 100% in 2018/19 gives total cumulative deductions of $3,000,000 (2016/17) + $7,000,000 (2017/18) + $55,000,000 combined by 2018/19 (remaining instalments on the first two payments, plus 100% of the third).

Illustration ??Specified IP Right deduction spread to match remaining protection DIPN 49

A copyright costing $400,000 has protection expiring in YA2022/23. Rather than the standard 5 equal instalments, the deduction is compressed into the 4 years actually remaining: $100,000/year for YA2019/20??022/23.

Depreciation & Allowances

Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), Part 6 & related provisions ??capital allowances replace accounting depreciation for tax purposes; accounting depreciation itself is never deductible (IRO s.17(1)(c)).

Freshness noteThis combined file is a static snapshot. For live source freshness checks, open the original multi-page folder and regenerate assets/js/data-manifest.js.

1. Overview

Because capital expenditure and accounting depreciation are non-deductible under Profits Tax (s.17(1)(c)/(d)), Part 6 of the IRO substitutes a separate, statutory system of capital allowances for four categories of qualifying capital expenditure: (1) industrial buildings, (2) commercial buildings, (3) plant & machinery, and (4) a growing list of 100%/5-year write-off regimes for specific asset types (ss.16C??6L) that sit alongside, and interact with, Part 6.

Key mechanic ??add-back and allowance in the same computation In every profits tax computation: add back accounting depreciation (non-deductible), then deduct the correct statutory allowance instead. The two numbers are almost never equal ??this is the single most common adjustment in a Hong Kong tax computation.

2. Industrial & Commercial Buildings IRO ss.33A, 34, 36, 37

Building typeInitial allowanceAnnual allowanceBalancing allowance/charge
Industrial building or structure (mills, factories, docks, utility undertakings, qualifying storage/farming/R&D buildings)20% (1/5) of cost, year of first use4% (1/25) straight-line on costOn sale/lease termination/demolition ??balancing charge capped at total allowances previously granted
Commercial building or structure (all other non-excluded buildings)None4% (1/25) straight-line on cost, from 1998/99Same balancing mechanism as industrial buildings, from 1998/99

"Industrial building or structure" is narrowly defined (DIPN 2) ??it excludes dwellings, retail shops, showrooms, hotels, and offices. Where a building is used partly for a qualifying industrial purpose and partly not, a 10% de-minimis rule applies: if the non-qualifying use is no more than 10% of the whole, the building is treated as wholly industrial. On a sale, the buyer's future annual allowance is recalculated as the residue of expenditure after sale, spread over the remaining years to the building's 25th year from first use.

Historical note Before 1998/99, commercial buildings only qualified for a much smaller "rebuilding allowance" (0.75% pre-1990/91, then 2% from 1990/91??997/98) rather than the modern 4% annual allowance ??relevant only when tracing the residue of expenditure on a very old building still being written down today.

3. Plant & Machinery ??Pooling System IRO ss.39B??9E

Since YA1980/81, plant and machinery of the same annual-allowance rate class is pooled together rather than tracked asset-by-asset (the old asset-by-asset method under ss.37??9 survives only for hire-purchase assets, mixed business/private-use assets, and a few other special cases).

Initial allowance60% of cost, given once on acquisition
Annual allowanceReducing-balance on the pool's "reducing value," at 10%, 20% or 30% depending on asset class (Board of Inland Revenue rates)
Common annual allowance rate classes (Appendix A(i), DIPN 7)
RateAsset examples
10%Air-conditioning plant, lifts, cables, ships
20%Furniture, fixtures, domestic/room air-conditioners ??the general residual rate for unlisted items
30%Motor vehicles, computer/EDP equipment, textile/electronics manufacturing plant, tractors/bulldozers

Balancing allowances only arise when the trade/pool ceases altogether. Balancing charges can arise at any time disposal proceeds exceed the pool's reducing value, but are capped at the total allowances previously granted for that asset (you can never be charged more than you were originally allowed). Hire-purchase assets, and assets moved between private and business use, are excluded from pooling and calculated separately with notional allowance adjustments.

5. Patents, Know-How & Specified IP Rights IRO ss.16E, 16EA??6EC

AssetBasisTreatment
Patent rights / know-hows.16E100% one-off, year of purchase ??no spreading

Expenditure deductible under s.16E/16EA/16G/16I is excluded from Part 6 depreciation allowances ??the regimes are mutually exclusive per asset, not cumulative. Anti-avoidance carve-outs deny the deduction for sale-and-license-back arrangements, purchases from associates, and IP used exclusively outside Hong Kong by another person under a leveraged licensing structure.

IRD Administration

Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) ??filing, assessment, objections, appeals, provisional tax and penalty mechanics that apply across Profits, Property and Salaries Tax.

Source note The section numbers on this page reflect the IRO's long-standing, stable administration framework, but this build's local CAP112 text extraction did not cleanly surface the Part 9??4 body text for direct verification (see 禮7). 1. Overview

These provisions are common infrastructure across Profits Tax, Property Tax and Salaries Tax ??a computation is only complete once you know how it gets filed, assessed, and (if needed) disputed or paid provisionally.

2. Returns & Filing IRO s.51

The Commissioner issues annual returns (profits tax, salaries tax, property tax, employer's return) which must be completed and filed by the stated due date ??extensions are routinely available for corporate profits tax returns filed through a tax representative under the Block Extension Scheme. Every person carrying on a trade, profession or business must keep sufficient records (s.51C) for at least 7 years to enable profits/losses to be readily ascertained.

Practical note For a newly incorporated company, the first profits tax return is usually issued around 18 months after incorporation or commencement of business ??but a company must still notify the Commissioner of chargeability (s.51(2)) within 4 months after the end of the basis period if no return has been issued, even where no return has yet been received.

3. Assessment & Estimated Assessment IRO ss.59??0

The assessor assesses the return as filed, or ??where no return is filed, or the assessor is not satisfied with a return ??issues an estimated assessment based on the best information available. Estimated assessments are commonly issued precisely to protect the statutory time limit for assessment where a return is outstanding; they are not a final determination and are superseded once the actual figures are agreed (usually via an objection, 禮4).

Additional assessments may be raised within the statutory time limit (generally 6 years from the end of the relevant year of assessment, extended to 10 years in cases involving fraud or wilful default) where profits/income have been understated or under-assessed.

4. Objections & Appeals IRO s.64 (objections), s.66 (Board of Review), s.69 (case stated)

StageTime limitForum
Notice of objection1 month from the date of the assessmentCommissioner of Inland Revenue
Appeal against the Commissioner's determination1 month from the determinationBoard of Review
Case stated appeal on a point of law1 month from the Board's decisionCourt of First Instance, then Court of Appeal / Court of Final Appeal

An objection must state precisely the grounds of objection ??a bare, unparticularised objection can be rejected as invalid. Tax under dispute is not automatically suspended; the taxpayer must separately apply to hold over payment of the tax in dispute (or the portion in dispute) pending resolution.

5. Provisional Tax & Holdover IRO ss.63A??3U, 71

Provisional profits tax, provisional salaries tax, and provisional property tax are each charged in advance, generally based on the immediately preceding year's assessable profits/income, and later set off against the final assessment for that year once it issues. A taxpayer may apply to hold over payment of the whole or part of provisional tax where, for example, the current year's profits/income are objectively likely to be significantly lower than the prior year's, or personal allowances have increased, or an objection is pending on the immediately preceding year's assessment.

Practical note Holdover applications have their own strict statutory deadlines (broadly, the later of 28 days before the provisional tax is due, or 14 days after the provisional tax notice is issued) ??missing the window forfeits the holdover regardless of the merits.

6. Penalties IRO ss.80, 82, 82A

ContraventionConsequence
Failure to file a return, or filing late, without reasonable excuseSummary conviction fine and/or additional tax (s.80)
Making an incorrect return, or omitting income, without reasonable excuseAdditional tax up to treble the tax undercharged (s.82A) ??an administrative penalty assessed by the Commissioner, distinct from criminal prosecution
Wilfully with intent to evade, or assisting another to evade, taxCriminal prosecution (s.82) ??fine up to treble the tax undercharged plus imprisonment up to 3 years

Additional tax under s.82A is an administrative sanction (no need to prove criminal intent) and can be settled by way of a compound penalty offer from the Commissioner in less serious cases ??this is the mechanism most commonly encountered in routine tax computation corrections and voluntary disclosures.

7. Double Taxation Relief & Tax Clearance IRO ss.49, 50, 51D

Where Hong Kong has a comprehensive double taxation agreement (CDTA) with another jurisdiction, s.50 provides a tax credit for foreign tax paid on income also taxed in Hong Kong (interacting with the s.16(1)(c)/(2J) deduction restriction on the Profits Tax page). Employers must comply with tax clearance obligations (s.51D) before an employee who is about to leave Hong Kong permanently, or for a substantial period, ceases employment ??including withholding final payments until IRD confirms clearance or a specified period has elapsed.

Illustration ??Interest deduction restricted by a tax-free secured deposit DIPN 13A

Taxpayer F borrows $1,000,000 at 5% interest, secured by a $1,000,000 deposit earning tax-exempt interest at 4%. Illustration ??Service company "Type II" management fee cap DIPN 24

A professional firm pays $4,000,000 in management fees to a related service company whose actual attributable cost of providing those services is $3,000,000. The deduction is capped at cost plus a 12.5% mark-up: $3,000,000 ? 112.5% = $3,375,000. The $625,000 excess is added back, increasing assessable profits from $1,000,000 (per accounts) to $1,625,000.

Illustration ??Foreign tax deductible only where no DTA credit is available DIPN 28

Bank-HK pays income tax on interest income in Jurisdiction F. If Hong Kong has no double tax agreement with Jurisdiction F, the foreign tax is deductible under s.16(1)(c). If Hong Kong does have a DTA with Jurisdiction F, s.16(2J) denies the deduction ??Bank-HK must instead claim a tax credit under s.50, since double relief (deduction plus credit) is not permitted.

Illustration ??Hong Kong bank deposit interest exemption straddling the exemption date DIPN 34

A taxpayer has $10,000,000 trading profits plus $4,000,000 of local bank deposit interest, of which $1,000,000 accrued before 22 June 1998 (the exemption's effective date) and $3,000,000 accrued after. Only the pre-exemption $1,000,000 is taxable; total 1998/99 assessable profits = $11,000,000 rather than $14,000,000.

Illustration ??Prepaid expenses follow accounting treatment, not cash timing DIPN 40, following CIR v Secan

Following the Secan case, a full cash prepayment (e.g. 3 years' rent or insurance paid up front) is no longer deductible in full in the year of payment if the applicable accounting standard requires it to be amortised over the period it relates to ??the tax deduction now follows the accounting/matching treatment, reversing the Department's older cash-basis practice.

Structurally parallel but shorter (129 boxes ??~111) and reorganized. The headline differences:

  • Part 1 extras (boxes 4??): whether the proprietor/spouse/partner received emoluments or interest on capital from the business ??such payments are non-deductible under s.17(2), and box 5 confirms the tax computation adjusts for it. The two-tiered rate election is box 6 here, not box 4.

10. BIR54 ??What It Actually Is

Common misconception, corrected BIR54 is not a second "persons other than corporations" return. Its actual title is "Profits Tax Return ??In Respect of Non-Resident Persons." It is filed under ss.20A/20B by the Hong Kong resident payer or agent, in the non-resident's own name, to report that non-resident's Hong Kong-sourced trade/business profits ??most commonly royalty/IP payments, or income received via a Hong Kong agent. It's bilingual (Chinese/English) and far shorter than BIR51/52 (~22 boxes).

Key sections: a two-tiered rate election made on behalf of the non-resident (box 3, with the same connected-entity disclosure logic as BIR51/S1); an IP use/assignment block (boxes 11??7) determining whether the deemed-profit computation under s.21A(1)(a) applies 100% or 30% of the royalty receipt as assessable profit; a section on the agent's role in Hong Kong; and a reciprocal shipowner exemption block (boxes 18??0, s.23B(4A)) for non-resident shipowners.

1. Overview & Charging Basis IRO s.14

Hong Kong taxes profits on a territorial source basis, not on residence. Section 14(1) charges profits tax on every person (including a corporation) carrying on a trade, profession or business in Hong Kong, on assessable profits arising in or derived from Hong Kong for that trade/profession/business ??profits from the sale of capital assets are expressly excluded from the charge.

Statutory text ??s.14(1) "...profits tax shall be charged for each year of assessment on every person carrying on a trade, profession or business in Hong Kong in respect of his assessable profits arising in or derived from Hong Kong for that year from such trade, profession or business (excluding profits arising from the sale of capital assets) as ascertained in accordance with this Part."

Three conditions must all be met for a charge to arise: (1) the person carries on a trade, profession or business in Hong Kong; (2) the trade/profession/business produces profits other than from the sale of capital assets; (3) the profits are sourced in Hong Kong. Locality of profits is a question of fact determined by what the taxpayer did to earn the profit and where ??see DIPN 21 (Locality of Profits) for the "broad guiding principle" drawn from Hang Seng Bank, HK-TVB International, Kwong Mile, and other leading cases.

5. Basis Period & Loss Relief IRO ss.18, 18C, 19C

Assessable profits are normally computed on the accounting year ending within the year preceding the year of assessment (s.18(1)). Where a business commences or ceases partway through a year, ss.18C??8H set special opening/closing basis periods. Trading losses are carried forward indefinitely and set off against future assessable profits from the same source (s.19C) ??there is no time limit, but no carry-back and no group relief between separate legal entities in Hong Kong (each company is assessed on its own profits).

Where a taxpayer has both "normal" and "concessionary rate" trading receipts (e.g. under a qualifying debt instrument or reinsurance concession), s.19CA/19CB impose a cross-set-off formula using a statutory adjustment factor rather than a straight one-for-one offset ??see DIPN 8.

6. Foreign-Sourced Income Exemption (FSIE) Part 4, Division 3A

Division 3A (ss.15H onward, added by the Inland Revenue (Amendment) (Taxation on Specified Foreign-sourced Income) Ordinance 2022, effective 1 January 2023) deems specified categories of foreign-sourced income ??interest, dividends, disposal gains on equity interests, and IP income ??received in Hong Kong by an MNE entity to be Hong Kong-sourced unless the entity meets an economic substance requirement (for interest/dividends/disposal gains) or complies with the nexus approach (for IP income), or qualifies for the participation exemption (for dividends/disposal gains on substantial equity interests).

Practical effect A Hong Kong holding or treasury company receiving offshore dividends, interest, or IP royalties that would otherwise be treated as offshore (non-taxable) under the ordinary source principle may now be taxed on that income unless it can document adequate economic substance in Hong Kong or a qualifying participation/nexus position. This is a common trap in tax computations for group treasury/holding entities ??always check FSIE before treating offshore passive income as automatically non-taxable.

1. Overview & Charging Basis IRO s.5

Property tax is charged for each year of assessment on every person who owns land or buildings in Hong Kong, computed at the standard rate on the net assessable value ??regardless of whether the owner carries on any trade or business. It is a tax on ownership + letting, not on trading profit as such.

Statutory text ??s.5(1) "Property tax shall...be charged for each year of assessment on every person being the owner of any land or buildings or land and buildings wherever situate in Hong Kong and shall be computed at the standard rate on the net assessable value of such land or buildings..."
Current rate
Standard rate applied to net assessable value15%

Owner-occupied property generates no rental consideration and therefore has no assessable value ??no property tax arises. Where the owner of the land is not the owner of the buildings on it, separate assessments are made for each (s.5(1)(b)).

2. Net Assessable Value IRO s.5(1A), s.5B, s.7C

Assessable value (s.5B(2)) is the consideration in money or money's worth payable for the right to use the land/buildings during the year ??i.e. rent and other consideration receivable, not merely received. Net assessable value (s.5(1A)) then deducts:

Assessable value (rent/consideration receivable for the year)X
Less: irrecoverable consideration proved bad during the year (s.7C)(X)
Less: rates paid by the owner, if the owner agreed to bear them(X)

The 20% repairs/outgoings allowance is flat and statutory ??it applies regardless of the owner's actual repair costs, and cannot be varied by claiming higher actual expenditure. Consideration recovered in a later year, having previously been written off as irrecoverable under s.7C, is brought back into that later year's assessable value (s.7C(2)).

Lease premiums paid up front are deemed under s.5B(3)/(4) to be payable in equal monthly instalments spread over the period of the right of use, capped at a maximum of 3 years ??even if the actual lease term is longer. See the DIPN 4/DIPN 14 illustrations below.

4. Corporate Interaction with Profits Tax IRO s.25, s.5(2)(a)

For a corporation carrying on a trade, profession or business in Hong Kong, property tax and profits tax can otherwise double-tax the same rental income. Two relief mechanisms apply:

  • Set-off (s.25): where the rental profits form part of the corporation's trading profits (or the property is used to produce chargeable profits), profits tax payable is reduced by the property tax already paid on that property. If property tax paid exceeds the profits tax payable, the excess is refunded (s.25, proviso (b), via s.79).
  • Upfront exemption (s.5(2)(a)): instead of paying property tax and later claiming the set-off, a corporation entitled to the s.25 set-off may apply in writing to the Commissioner for the property to be exempted from property tax altogether. The corporation must notify the Commissioner within 30 days of any change in ownership/use affecting the exemption (s.5(2)(c)).
Practical note Non-corporate owners (individuals, partnerships) do not get the s.25 set-off/exemption against profits tax in the same way ??they may instead elect Personal Assessment (IRO s.41) to have property tax effectively absorbed into a single personal-assessment computation where advantageous. See the Salaries Tax page for Personal Assessment.

1. Overview & Charging Basis IRO s.8

Salaries tax is charged on income arising in or derived from Hong Kong from an office, employment of profit, or pension (s.8(1)) ??again a territorial-source tax, not a residence-based one. 1. Overview

Stamp duty is a transaction tax, not an income tax ??it is charged on specific categories of instruments (documents), not on profit. The three main heads for a Hong Kong company are: (1) Hong Kong stock transfers, (2) immovable property conveyances/agreements, and (3) leases. Hong Kong has no capital gains tax, estate duty, gift tax, or VAT ??stamp duty is the main transaction-based levy filling that gap.

7. Partnership Admission & Retirement

Where a partner is admitted to, or retires from, a partnership that holds Hong Kong stock or immovable property, the change in each partner's proportionate interest can itself be a chargeable "sale and purchase" of the underlying property/stock for stamp duty purposes (the incoming/continuing partners are treated as acquiring the outgoing/diluted partners' fractional interest). This is assessed using the same AVD/stock-transfer rules above, applied to the fractional interest that changes hands ??always check partnership deeds and capital-account movements for a hidden stamp duty trigger whenever partners change.

Module 9 Extra Practice Q&A

Additional paraphrased illustrations and exam-style questions based on the HKICPA Module 9 Principles of Taxation textbook, 4th edition. These are study notes, not copied textbook answers. Check the current IRO, SDO, Budget measures, and IRD guidance before using any figure in a filing position.

1. Profits Tax - Trading, Source, Deductions

Illustration PT-1 Badges of trade

Question. A company buys a shop unit with short-term bank finance, renovates it lightly, advertises it for resale within three months, and sells at a gain. Is the gain likely capital or trading?

中文題目：一間公司以短期銀行貸款購入商舖，簡單裝修後三個月內放售並賺取收益。該收益較可能屬資本性質，還是營業買賣收益？

Answer. It is likely trading. Short holding period, financing arranged for quick turnover, resale activity, and commercial organization all point toward an adventure in the nature of trade. The label in board minutes is useful evidence, but the whole fact pattern matters more.

中文答案：較可能屬營業買賣收益。持有期短、以短期融資支持快速轉售、有主動放售行為及商業組織安排，都指向「屬貿易性質的一次性交易」。董事會紀錄的說法有參考價值，但最終仍要整體看事實。

Illustration PT-2 Capital asset disposal

Question. A retailer buys a warehouse for storage, uses it for several years, then sells it after receiving an unsolicited offer. Is the gain automatically taxable because the seller is a business?

中文題目：零售商購入倉庫作儲貨用途，使用數年後因收到買家主動出價而出售。是否因賣方是商業機構，收益便自動須課利得稅？

Answer. No. A business can hold capital assets. If the warehouse was acquired and used as a fixed capital facility rather than trading stock, the gain is more likely capital and outside profits tax, subject to the facts.

中文答案：不是。商業機構也可以持有資本資產。如果倉庫購入及使用目的均屬固定資本設施，而非買賣存貨，出售收益較可能屬資本性質，不屬利得稅課稅範圍；但仍須視乎全部事實。

Illustration PT-3 Source of service income

Question. A Hong Kong company signs contracts in Hong Kong but its staff perform all consultancy fieldwork in Japan. Where should the source analysis focus?

中文題目：香港公司在香港簽約，但員工全部顧問實地工作均在日本進行。判斷利潤來源地時應集中看甚麼？

Answer. Focus on what the taxpayer did to earn the fee and where those profit-producing operations took place. For service income, performance of the services usually carries strong weight; contract signing alone is not always decisive.

中文答案：重點是納稅人做了甚麼來賺取該收入，以及產生利潤的實際操作在何處進行。服務收入通常很重視服務履行地；單靠簽約地點未必具決定性。

Illustration PT-4 Bad debt deduction

Question. A trading debt of HK$90,000 was previously booked as taxable sales income and is later written off as irrecoverable. A separate staff loan of HK$40,000 is also written off. Which write-off is deductible?

中文題目：一筆 HK$90,000 貿易應收款曾計入應課稅銷售收入，後來證實不能收回並撇銷；另有 HK$40,000 員工貸款亦撇銷。哪一項可扣稅？

Answer. The trading debt is deductible because it arose from taxable trading receipts already brought into account. The staff loan is normally not deductible for an ordinary trader because making staff loans is not its money-lending business and the loan principal was not taxable income.

中文答案：貿易壞帳可扣稅，因為它源自已入帳的應課稅營業收入。員工貸款一般不可扣稅，因普通貿易公司並非經營放債業務，而貸款本金亦從未作為應課稅收入入帳。

Question PT-5 Quick MCQ

Question. Which expense is most clearly deductible for profits tax?

中文題目：以下哪項開支最明顯可作利得稅扣除？

A. Private rent of the managing director. B. Capital cost of acquiring a liquor licence. C. Interest on a commercial loan used to buy trading stock, assuming the interest rules are met. D. Cost recovered from an insurer.

Answer. C. The borrowing is for trading stock and can satisfy the production-of-profits test, but interest must still pass the specific interest deduction conditions.

中文答案：C。借款用於購買營業存貨，較能符合「產生應課稅利潤」的要求；但利息仍須通過利息扣除的特定條件。

2. Salaries Tax - Employment Income and Benefits

Question ST-1 Employment vs self-employment

Question. A marketing worker has fixed office hours, uses the company's equipment, reports to a director, and receives a monthly salary plus commission. Salaries tax or profits tax?

中文題目：市場推廣人員有固定辦公時間，使用公司設備，向董事匯報，並收取月薪加佣金。應較可能屬薪俸稅還是利得稅？

Answer. Salaries tax is more likely. Control, integration into the employer's organization, and lack of entrepreneurial risk point to employment rather than an independent profession or business.

中文答案：較可能屬薪俸稅。受僱主控制、融入僱主組織、缺乏自行承擔營商風險，均指向僱傭關係而非獨立專業或業務。

Question ST-2 Contract for services

Question. A consultant serves several clients, rents her own office, hires assistants, bears overheads, and invoices fixed project fees. What tax head is more likely?

中文題目：一名顧問服務多名客戶，自租辦公室、聘請助理、自行承擔營運開支，並按項目收費。較可能屬哪一稅種？

Answer. Profits tax is more likely. She appears to be carrying on a profession or business on her own account rather than holding an employment.

中文答案：較可能屬利得稅。她看似以自己名義經營專業或業務，而非受僱於單一僱主。

Question ST-3 Share option timing

Question. An employee receives an employment-related share option and later exercises it at a discount. When is the benefit normally measured?

中文題目：僱員因受僱關係取得股份期權，其後以折讓價行使。通常何時計算應課稅利益？

Answer. The taxable benefit is generally measured when the option is exercised, released, or assigned, depending on the event, with the gain computed by reference to the relevant market value and exercise price rules.

中文答案：一般在期權被行使、放棄或轉讓時，按相關事件計算應課稅利益，通常會參考市值與行使價等規則計算收益。

3. Property Tax - Rental Income and Corporate Owners

Illustration PrT-1 Tenant-paid expenses

Question. A tenant pays HK$20,000 repair costs that the landlord was obliged to bear under the lease. Is the payment ignored for property tax?

中文題目：租客支付 HK$20,000 修葺費，而根據租約該費用本應由業主承擔。物業稅是否可忽略該款項？

Answer. No. Where the tenant discharges an obligation of the owner, the amount can be treated as consideration for use of the property. The statutory 20% allowance then applies in computing net assessable value.

中文答案：不可忽略。若租客代業主履行責任，該款項可被視為使用物業的代價，然後在計算應評稅淨值時扣除 20% 法定修葺免稅額。

Illustration PrT-2 Simple NAV computation

Question. Annual rent is HK$360,000. Rates of HK$12,000 are paid by the owner. No irrecoverable rent. Compute net assessable value.

中文題目：全年租金 HK$360,000，差餉 HK$12,000 由業主支付，沒有不能追回租金。計算應評稅淨值。

Assessable value before allowance: 360,000 - 12,000 = 348,000
Less statutory repairs allowance: 348,000 x 20% = 69,600
Net assessable value: 278,400

Answer. NAV is HK$278,400.

中文答案：應評稅淨值為 HK$278,400。

Question PrT-3 Corporate property owner

Question. A Hong Kong company lets out an office and the rental income is already included in profits tax. Can it avoid double tax under property tax?

中文題目：香港公司出租辦公室，租金收入已納入利得稅課稅。可否避免再繳物業稅？

Answer. A corporation can usually seek exemption from property tax where the property income is chargeable to profits tax. The rental stream is then dealt with under profits tax rather than being taxed twice.

中文答案：公司如相關物業收入已須繳利得稅，通常可申請豁免物業稅，避免同一租金收入被重複課稅。

4. Personal Assessment - Election and Loss Set-off

Illustration PA-1 Mortgage interest limit

Question. Under personal assessment, an individual has property NAV of HK$60,000 and interest of HK$75,000 on the loan used to buy that income-producing property. How much interest can be deducted against that property income?

中文題目：在個人入息課稅下，某人有物業應評稅淨值 HK$60,000，購買該收租物業的貸款利息為 HK$75,000。可扣除多少利息？

Answer. HK$60,000. The property-loan interest deduction is limited by the net assessable value of the relevant property included in personal assessment.

中文答案：HK$60,000。可扣除的物業貸款利息以納入個人入息課稅的相關物業應評稅淨值為上限。

Illustration PA-2 Business loss limit

Question. Total income after allowable deductions becomes negative because of concessionary deductions and a business loss. How much personal assessment loss can be carried forward?

中文題目：扣除特惠扣除及業務虧損後，總入息變成負數。可結轉的個人入息課稅虧損是多少？

Answer. The carry-forward personal assessment loss is limited to the business loss element. A deficit caused by personal allowances or non-business concessionary deductions is not carried forward as a business loss.

中文答案：可結轉的個人入息課稅虧損以業務虧損部分為限。由個人免稅額或非業務性特惠扣除造成的不足額，不會作為業務虧損結轉。

Question PA-3 Married couple election

Question. A married person wants personal assessment, but the spouse also has income chargeable to tax. Can one spouse elect alone?

中文題目：已婚人士想選擇個人入息課稅，但配偶亦有應課稅收入。一方可否單獨選擇？

Answer. Generally no. Where both spouses have income within the personal assessment framework, joint election is required. This is important when modelling whether personal assessment is beneficial.

中文答案：一般不可以。若夫婦雙方均有屬個人入息課稅框架內的收入，通常需要共同選擇。判斷個人入息課稅是否有利時必須留意這點。

5. Stamp Duty - Instruments, Property, Shares, Relief

Question SD-1 Instrument-based tax

Question. Why is it imprecise to say that stamp duty taxes "transactions"?

中文題目：為何說印花稅是對「交易」課稅並不精確？

Answer. Stamp duty is charged on instruments. A transaction matters because it produces or explains the instrument, but the charge is imposed by reference to the stampable document and the relevant charging head.

中文答案：印花稅是對文書課稅。交易本身重要，是因為它產生或解釋該文書；但真正課稅基礎是可予徵收印花稅的文件及相關課稅項目。

Illustration SD-2 Hong Kong stock transfer

Question. Hong Kong shares are sold for HK$100,000 but market value is HK$120,000. Which amount is used for ad valorem stamp duty?

中文題目：香港股票以 HK$100,000 出售，但市值為 HK$120,000。從價印花稅應以哪個金額計算？

Answer. HK$120,000. For Hong Kong stock transfers, duty is generally computed on the higher of consideration and value. Check the current stock duty rate before filing, as rates have changed over time.

中文答案：HK$120,000。香港股票轉讓一般按代價及價值兩者中較高者計算印花稅。由於股票印花稅率曾有變動，實際申報前應查核現行稅率。

Question SD-3 Associated company relief

Question. ParentCo transfers Hong Kong immovable property to a 100%-owned subsidiary. What relief may be relevant?

中文題目：母公司把香港不動產轉讓予其 100% 擁有的附屬公司。可能涉及哪項印花稅寬免？

Answer. Associated-company relief under SDO s.45 may be relevant if the ownership threshold and continuity conditions are satisfied. The parties must monitor the post-transfer association period because relief can be clawed back if the conditions break.

中文答案：如符合持股比例及持續關聯條件，可能適用《印花稅條例》第 45 條關聯公司寬免。轉讓後仍須監察關聯關係維持期，否則寬免可能被撤回。

6. Cross-border Profits Tax - Withholding and Non-residents

Question CB-1 Non-resident entertainer

Question. A Hong Kong event organizer pays a non-resident performer's company for a Hong Kong performance. Why might Hong Kong tax be withheld even if the performer is paid through a company?

中文題目：香港活動主辦方向非居民表演者的公司支付香港演出費。即使款項經公司支付，為何仍可能需要在香港預扣稅？

Answer. The IRO contains deeming and collection rules for non-resident entertainers and sportspeople. Interposing a company does not necessarily remove the Hong Kong charge where the performance activity is in Hong Kong.

中文答案：《稅務條例》對非居民演藝人員及運動員有視作收入及徵收規則。即使中間有公司收款，只要演出活動在香港進行，未必能避免香港稅務責任。

Illustration CB-2 Deemed profit method

Question. If the law taxes a non-resident entertainer on a deemed profit basis, what are the two moving parts in the computation?

中文題目：若法例以視作利潤方法向非居民表演者課稅，計算時主要有哪兩個變動部分？

Answer. First identify the gross payment that falls within the deeming rule. Then apply the statutory deemed-profit fraction and the applicable tax rate. If the actual-profit method is claimed, only properly connected deductible expenses should be used.

中文答案：第一，確定受視作規則涵蓋的總付款；第二，套用法定視作利潤比例及適用稅率。如改用實際利潤方法，只應扣除與香港演出適當相關的可扣稅開支。
