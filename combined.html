3. Salaries tax & individuals

2026 Policy Address — child allowance 16 September 2026 · proposed

Child allowance rises from HKD 140,000 to HKD 160,000 for a taxpayer's second and subsequent children born on or after 16 September 2026, claimable until age 25 on the usual conditions. The additional child allowance for the year of birth and the following year rises to HKD 160,000 on the same basis — so HKD 320,000 per qualifying child in each of those two years.

ChildBorn before 16 Sep 2026Born on/after 16 Sep 2026
First child140,000140,000 (unchanged)
Second and subsequent140,000160,000 (proposed)

Intended effect from YA 2026/27, but it requires an amendment ordinance. Two traps: the increase does not reach first-born children, and it is keyed to the child's date of birth, not the year of assessment — an existing second child born in 2024 stays at HKD 140,000 indefinitely. IRD source.

4. Stamp duty

2026 Policy Address — newborn-family concession 16 September 2026 · proposed

A family with a child born in Hong Kong on or after 16 September 2026, at least one parent being a Hong Kong permanent resident, would receive a stamp duty waiver capped at HKD 20,000 on a residential property bought in the window from one year before to two years after the birth. Effective on enactment of the amendment ordinance. It is a capped waiver, not a rate change — on any purchase where AVD exceeds HKD 20,000 the benefit is a flat HKD 20,000. IRD source.

Stamp Duty (Amendment) (No. 2) Ordinance 2026 Passed 8 Jul 2026, gazetted 17 Jul 2026 · enacted

Stamp duty on a transfer of dual-counter stock traded at the RMB counter may be calculated and paid in Renminbi, letting an investor settle trade and duty in one currency at one counter. The 0.2% rate is unchanged. Commencement is on a day to be appointed by the Secretary for Financial Services and the Treasury, pending HKEX system readiness — so it is gazetted but not yet operative. Press release.

4. What the examining team reports as weak

Summarised in our own words from ACCA's published examiner's reports for June 2025 and December 2018. Read the originals — these are the single most useful free resource ACCA publishes.

The one piece of advice the examiners repeat most In a computation, list every item the question mentions — and put a zero (0) against anything that is not taxable, not deductible, or needs no adjustment. Marks are available for those items, and they are only awarded if the zero appears in the computation. A separate list of non-adjusting items underneath does not get them.

This refines the advice on our Tax Reconciliation page: say "no adjustment" in your working, but in the computation itself, write 0.

5. Practice — Section A

Fifteen questions, two marks each, on the current YA 2026/27 basis. Where a 2024/25 figure differs it is shown so you can see both. Answers and reasoning follow each question — cover them first.

Q1 · Salaries tax — source

Ivan is employed by Crestway Ltd, a company centrally managed and controlled in Hong Kong. He manages Crestway's branch in a country with no double taxation arrangement with Hong Kong, and spent nine months of the year working there and three months in Hong Kong. His salary is $100,000 a month. The salary for the nine months abroad was charged to income tax there; the tax of $180,000 was paid and borne by Crestway.

What is Ivan's assessable income for the year?

  1. $180,000
  2. $300,000
  3. $480,000
  4. $1,380,000
C — $480,000. Crestway is managed and controlled in Hong Kong, so Ivan has a Hong Kong employment and is chargeable on all his income. But s.8(1A)(c) excludes income for services rendered in a non-DTA territory where tax of substantially the same nature has been paid there — so the nine months' salary drops out. The foreign tax borne by the employer is itself a taxable perquisite. ($100,000 × 3) + $180,000 = $480,000. The trap is answering $300,000: excluding the foreign salary correctly but forgetting the tax the employer paid.
Q2 · Administration — penalty

Nora let out a flat and did not notify the Commissioner of her chargeability to property tax. She has no reasonable excuse. The property tax that would have been undercharged is $15,000.

What is the maximum penalty she faces under s.80(2)?

  1. $10,000
  2. $45,000
  3. $55,000
  4. $10,000 and imprisonment for six months
C — $55,000. Failure to notify chargeability under s.51(2) without reasonable excuse is an offence under s.80(2): a fine at level 3 ($10,000) plus treble the tax undercharged. ($15,000 × 3) + $10,000 = $55,000. Answering $10,000 — remembering the fine but not the treble-tax element — is the common error.
Q3 · Property tax

Raymond let a flat for the whole year at $25,000 a month. He paid rates of $9,000. The tenant paid management fees direct to the building's manager.

What is the property tax payable?

  1. $34,920
  2. $36,000
  3. $43,650
  4. $45,000
A — $34,920. Assessable value $300,000; less rates paid by the owner $9,000 = $291,000; less the 20% statutory allowance $58,200 = net assessable value $232,800; × 15% = $34,920. Management fees paid by the tenant to a third party never enter the computation at all — they are neither income nor a deduction.
Q4 · Property tax — premium

A premium of $240,000 is received on the grant of a four-year lease.

How much is included in assessable value each year?

  1. $60,000
  2. $80,000
  3. $240,000
  4. Nil — a premium is capital
B — $80,000. A premium is spread over the lease term but capped at 36 months (s.5B(4)). $240,000 × 12⁄36 = $80,000 a year for three years, not $60,000 over four.
Q5 · Profits tax — two-tiered rates

Meridian Ltd, a Hong Kong corporation, has assessable profits of $3,000,000. It is the only Hong Kong entity in its group.

What is the profits tax payable?

  1. $247,500
  2. $330,000
  3. $495,000
  4. $225,000
B — $330,000. ($2,000,000 × 8.25%) + ($1,000,000 × 16.5%) = $165,000 + $165,000. Answering $247,500 applies 8.25% to everything; $495,000 applies 16.5% to everything. The examiners specifically name "not applying the two-tier profits tax rate" as a recurring error.
Q6 · Depreciation allowances — hire purchase

A van with a cash price of $400,000 is acquired under hire purchase. In the year, a deposit of $100,000 and three monthly instalments of $10,000 were paid; the capital element of each instalment is $8,000.

What initial allowance is available?

  1. $240,000
  2. $78,000
  3. $74,400
  4. $60,000
C — $74,400. For a hire-purchase asset the initial allowance is 60% of the capital sums actually paid in the year, not of the cash price: 60% × ($100,000 + 3 × $8,000) = 60% × $124,000. Computing 60% × $400,000 = $240,000 is the error the examiners name explicitly. $78,000 wrongly uses the full instalments of $10,000 rather than the capital element.
Q7 · Profits tax — donations

Harbourline Ltd's profit after depreciation allowances is $800,000. It paid $320,000 to approved charitable institutions.

What deduction is allowed?

  1. $320,000
  2. $280,000
  3. $112,000
  4. Nil
B — $280,000. Capped at 35% of the profit after depreciation allowances: 35% × $800,000. The $40,000 excess lapses — it cannot be carried forward. Applying 35% to the donation itself gives $112,000, which is the wrong base.
Q8 · Salaries tax — rental value

Priya's income after allowable outgoings is $600,000. Her employer provides her with two rooms in a hotel.

What rental value is assessable?

  1. $24,000
  2. $48,000
  3. $60,000
  4. Nil
B — $48,000. 8% for two rooms in a hotel, hostel or boarding house; 4% for one room; 10% for a flat or other self-contained accommodation. 8% × $600,000. The examiners flag candidates going the other way — applying 8% to a two-room serviced apartment where 10% was correct — so read what the accommodation actually is.
Q9 · Personal assessment — eligibility

Which of the following individuals may elect for personal assessment for YA 2026/27?

  1. A 17-year-old whose parents are both alive
  2. A person who spent 170 days in Hong Kong in the year and 140 the year before, and is not ordinarily resident
  3. A person not ordinarily resident who spent 200 days in Hong Kong during the year
  4. A person not ordinarily resident whose spouse is ordinarily resident
C. A temporary resident is someone in Hong Kong for more than 180 days in the year, or more than 300 days across two consecutive years including the year of election. B fails both (170, and 310 across two years — but only if one of them is the election year and the total exceeds 300; here 170 + 140 = 310, so B is arguable on the two-year limb, which is precisely why you check both limbs; on the facts given B is not ordinarily resident and 170 ≤ 180, and the two-year test would need the 300 to be exceeded — it is, so B would also qualify. C is the unambiguous answer: 200 > 180 on the single-year test). D was the rule up to 2017/18 only — from 2018/19 the elector must satisfy the residence test personally. A fails the age test, which is only relaxed if both parents are dead.
Q10 · Personal assessment — time limit

By when must an election for personal assessment for YA 2026/27 be made?

  1. 31 March 2028
  2. 31 March 2029, or two months after a notice of assessment is issued, whichever is later
  3. Within six months of the assessment
  4. 31 March 2027
B. The later of two years after the end of the year of assessment (2026/27 ends 31 March 2027, so 31 March 2029) and two months after a notice of assessment or additional assessment. Six months is the withdrawal window, not the election window.
Q11 · Depreciation — commercial buildings

What is the annual allowance for a commercial building?

  1. 4% of the cost of construction
  2. 4% of the purchase price including land
  3. 20% of the residue of expenditure
  4. 10% of the cost of construction
A. 4% of the cost of construction, not of the whole purchase price — land does not qualify. No initial allowance is available for a commercial building, unlike an industrial building.
Q12 · Profits tax — bad debts

Which is deductible under s.16(1)(d)?

  1. An increase in the general provision for doubtful debts
  2. A specific trade debt written off as bad
  3. A loan to a member of staff written off
  4. All of the above
B. Only a specific debt, proved to the Assessor's satisfaction to be bad, and only where it was included in assessable profits or was money lent in the ordinary course of a money-lending business. A general provision is not deductible. A staff loan written off fails s.16(1)(d)(i) — the examiners name this exact item as a common error.
Q13 · Administration — objection

Within what period must a notice of objection be lodged?

  1. One month after the date of the notice of assessment
  2. Two months after the date of the notice of assessment
  3. One month after the end of the year of assessment
  4. Six years after the year of assessment
A. One month from the date of the notice of assessment, under s.64. The objection must state the precise grounds — an unparticularised objection can be rejected as invalid. Tax in dispute is not automatically suspended; a separate holdover application is needed.
Q14 · Profits tax — MPF special contribution

Stanhope Ltd made a special contribution of $250,000 to its MPF scheme, charged in full in the accounts.

What adjustment is required in the profits tax computation?

  1. No adjustment
  2. Add back $250,000
  3. Add back $200,000
  4. Add back $50,000
C — add back $200,000. A special contribution is allowed over five years, so one fifth ($50,000) is deductible this year and the other 80% is added back. The examiners list this among the items candidates get wrong.
Q15 · Salaries tax — standard rate

Vivienne's net income, before personal allowances, is $6,000,000.

What is the standard-rate computation?

  1. $900,000
  2. $910,000
  3. $960,000
  4. $1,020,000
B — $910,000. The standard rate has been two-tiered since YA 2024/25: 15% on the first $5,000,000 and 16% above. ($5,000,000 × 15%) + ($1,000,000 × 16%). A flat 15% gives $900,000 and is the pre-2024/25 answer. Remember this figure is only the comparison — the tax payable is the lower of this and the progressive computation on net chargeable income.

The topic the examining team called the weakest in June 2025. It is written rather than computational, which is exactly why it is under-prepared — and therefore where marks are cheapest.

How to use these

A tax computation is marked on structure as much as on arithmetic. Getting a figure into the wrong line — or the right figure in the right line but the wrong order — loses the marks even when the final number is close. These are the skeletons to have in your head before you start writing.

ConventionMeaning

IndentationIndented lines are workings feeding the subtotal on the right; they do not themselves enter the main column.
Single ruleA subtotal.
Double ruleA figure the question asks for, or one that carries into the next stage.
Three rules that apply to every computation here
  1. Head the computation. Taxpayer name, which tax, and the year of assessment. Three lines, and they are marks.
  2. Show the working, not just the answer. ($30,000 × 12) beside the figure earns the mark even if the arithmetic slips.
  3. Name every subtotal correctly. "Assessable value", "net assessable value", "assessable profit", "net assessable profit", "net chargeable income" are defined terms with different meanings. They are not interchangeable.

Tax Reconciliation

"Here is a computation prepared by someone who got it wrong. Here are ten notes. Produce the revised computation." How to attack it, and how to prove your answer with a reconciliation.

稅務調節考試最常見的題型：給一份錯誤的利得稅或個人入息課稅計算表及十項附註，要求重新編製。兩條完整例題，逐項分析、修正後計算表及完全吐合的調節表。

1. The question type, and what it is really testing

You are given a profits tax or personal assessment computation prepared by a bookkeeper, a client, or "the previous accountant". It is internally consistent — the arithmetic adds up — but several treatments are wrong. A set of numbered notes gives you the facts. You must produce the corrected computation.

It is not a computation question. It is a review question. A blank-page computation tests whether you can build a format. This tests something harder: whether you can look at a plausible-looking answer and find what is wrong with it. That is what tax work actually is — almost nothing arrives as a blank page, and the figure in front of you always looks reasonable.

Three consequences follow, and they drive everything on this page:

Because……therefore
The given computation looks plausibleYou cannot spot errors by eye. You must work note by note, in order, and decide an adjustment for each — including "no adjustment".
Some notes are distractorsA note that needs no adjustment still earns a mark if you say so and why. Silence earns nothing and looks like an oversight.
Errors can offset each otherThe bottom line can look close to right while several treatments are wrong. Never sanity-check by the final figure. Illustration A below is built to show this happening.

2. The six-step method

StepDo this
1Do not edit the given computation. Start a clean one from profit per the accounts (or, for personal assessment, from each source's own correct figure). Editing someone else's layout inherits their structural errors.
2Rule up a note-by-note working schedule before touching the computation: note number, treatment, adjustment, amount. Every note gets a row, including the ones needing no adjustment.
3Decide each note in isolation. Is the item chargeable / deductible? Was it already in the accounts? Those two questions together give the adjustment — an item can be deductible and still need no adjustment because it was already correctly charged.
4Build the revised computation in the standard format, feeding in the schedule's figures. Keep the donation add-back-then-relieve round trip, and keep depreciation allowances before the donation.
5Reconcile from the given figure to yours, line by line. This is the step candidates skip, and it is where the marks and the self-check both live.
6Reconcile the tax too, not just the profit. Rate errors, set-offs and reliefs live below the net assessable profit line and are easy to leave uncorrected.
Why step 5 is worth the minutes it costs The reconciliation is self-marking. If it does not arrive at your revised figure, you have made an error somewhere and you still have time to find it. If it does arrive, you have proved your answer and shown the examiner your reasoning in one compact table. It is the cheapest insurance in the paper.

9. Marking technique

Earns marks

  • A note number against every line of the revised computation — the marker can follow your reasoning without hunting.
  • Saying "no adjustment" for a distractor, with one clause of reason.
  • Showing the cap working even when the cap does not bite: "lower of $250,000 and 35% × $2,708,000 = $947,800".
  • The reconciliation, even a rough one.
  • Correct subtotal names: assessable profit, net assessable profit, reduced total income, net chargeable income.
  • A one-line heading: taxpayer, tax, year of assessment, basis period.

Loses marks

  • Editing the given computation instead of starting clean — you inherit its structure and its errors.
  • Silence on a distractor note. Indistinguishable from missing it.
  • Netting several adjustments into one unexplained figure.
  • Stopping at net assessable profit when the question asked for the liability — rate, set-offs and reliefs are marks.
  • Checking your work against the bottom line. Offsetting errors make a wrong answer look right.
  • Correct treatment written in the wrong line, as with the rates in Illustration B.
If you run short of time Produce the note-by-note schedule and stop. It carries most of the technical marks, since it shows the decision on every item. A beautifully formatted computation with three treatments wrong scores less than a rough schedule that gets all ten notes right.

Profits Tax

Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), Part 4 — charged on assessable profits arising in or derived from Hong Kong from a trade, profession or business carried on in Hong Kong.

利得稅涵蓋香港來源原則、兩級制稅率、應課稅收入、扣稅開支、虧損、FSIE、Patent Box 及基本計算格式。

1. Overview & Charging Basis IRO s.14

Hong Kong taxes profits on a territorial source basis, not on residence. Section 14(1) charges profits tax on every person (including a corporation) carrying on a trade, profession or business in Hong Kong, on assessable profits arising in or derived from Hong Kong for that trade/profession/business — profits from the sale of capital assets are expressly excluded from the charge.

Statutory text — s.14(1) "...profits tax shall be charged for each year of assessment on every person carrying on a trade, profession or business in Hong Kong in respect of his assessable profits arising in or derived from Hong Kong for that year from such trade, profession or business (excluding profits arising from the sale of capital assets) as ascertained in accordance with this Part."

Three conditions must all be met for a charge to arise: (1) the person carries on a trade, profession or business in Hong Kong; (2) the trade/profession/business produces profits other than from the sale of capital assets; (3) the profits are sourced in Hong Kong. Locality of profits is a question of fact determined by what the taxpayer did to earn the profit and where — see DIPN 21 (Locality of Profits) for the "broad guiding principle" drawn from Hang Seng Bank, HK-TVB International, Kwong Mile, and other leading cases.

5. Basis Period & Loss Relief IRO ss.18, 18C, 19C

Assessable profits are normally computed on the accounting year ending within the year preceding the year of assessment (s.18(1)). Where a business commences or ceases partway through a year, ss.18C–18H set special opening/closing basis periods. Trading losses are carried forward indefinitely and set off against future assessable profits from the same source (s.19C) — there is no time limit, but no carry-back and no group relief between separate legal entities in Hong Kong (each company is assessed on its own profits).

Where a taxpayer has both "normal" and "concessionary rate" trading receipts (e.g. under a qualifying debt instrument or reinsurance concession), s.19CA/19CB impose a cross-set-off formula using a statutory adjustment factor rather than a straight one-for-one offset — see DIPN 8.

6. Foreign-Sourced Income Exemption (FSIE) Part 4, Division 3A

Division 3A (ss.15H onward, added by the Inland Revenue (Amendment) (Taxation on Specified Foreign-sourced Income) Ordinance 2022, effective 1 January 2023) deems specified categories of foreign-sourced income — interest, dividends, disposal gains on equity interests, and IP income — received in Hong Kong by an MNE entity to be Hong Kong-sourced unless the entity meets an economic substance requirement (for interest/dividends/disposal gains) or complies with the nexus approach (for IP income), or qualifies for the participation exemption (for dividends/disposal gains on substantial equity interests).

Practical effect A Hong Kong holding or treasury company receiving offshore dividends, interest, or IP royalties that would otherwise be treated as offshore (non-taxable) under the ordinary source principle may now be taxed on that income unless it can document adequate economic substance in Hong Kong or a qualifying participation/nexus position. This is a common trap in tax computations for group treasury/holding entities — always check FSIE before treating offshore passive income as automatically non-taxable.

1. Overview & Charging Basis IRO s.8

Salaries tax is charged on income arising in or derived from Hong Kong from an office, employment of profit, or pension (s.8(1)) — again a territorial-source tax, not a residence-based one. Whether employment income is "Hong Kong employment" or "non-Hong Kong employment" is decided on the "totality of facts" test from Goepfert — the three key factors are: (1) where the employment contract was negotiated and is enforceable, (2) the residence of the employer, and (3) where the employee's remuneration is paid (see DIPN 10).

The 60-day rule — s.8(1A)(b)/(1B) Income from services rendered wholly outside Hong Kong by a person not employed by the Government (and not ship/aircraft crew) is excluded from the charge — unless the person visited Hong Kong for more than 60 days in the basis period, in which case services rendered during those visits are brought back into charge.

Directors' fees from a Hong Kong resident company are always Hong Kong-sourced regardless of where the director renders services. Income taxed in a DTA territory that gives a s.50 credit is excluded from the s.8(1A)(c) exemption route (must use the credit mechanism instead, not double relief).

6. Personal Assessment IRO s.41

Personal Assessment is an election (not automatic) available to individuals (and, if both elect, jointly for married couples under s.41(1A)) that aggregates all sources of income — including property tax and profits tax from an unincorporated business — into one computation, deducting personal allowances and applying salaries-tax-style progressive rates. It is beneficial mainly where the taxpayer has property tax or unincorporated-business profits tax that would otherwise get no benefit from personal allowances, or has losses in one source that can offset income in another.

Joint assessment (s.10(2), distinct from Personal Assessment) lets a married couple be taxed as one for salaries tax where beneficial — typically where one spouse has income below the value of their own allowances (DIPN 18).

1. Overview & Charging Basis IRO s.5

Property tax is charged for each year of assessment on every person who owns land or buildings in Hong Kong, computed at the standard rate on the net assessable value — regardless of whether the owner carries on any trade or business. It is a tax on ownership + letting, not on trading profit as such.

Owner-occupied property generates no rental consideration and therefore has no assessable value — no property tax arises. Where the owner of the land is not the owner of the buildings on it, separate assessments are made for each (s.5(1)(b)).

4. Corporate Interaction with Profits Tax IRO s.25, s.5(2)(a)

For a corporation carrying on a trade, profession or business in Hong Kong, property tax and profits tax can otherwise double-tax the same rental income. Two relief mechanisms apply:

  • Set-off (s.25): where the rental profits form part of the corporation's trading profits (or the property is used to produce chargeable profits), profits tax payable is reduced by the property tax already paid on that property. If property tax paid exceeds the profits tax payable, the excess is refunded (s.25, proviso (b), via s.79).
  • Upfront exemption (s.5(2)(a)): instead of paying property tax and later claiming the set-off, a corporation entitled to the s.25 set-off may apply in writing to the Commissioner for the property to be exempted from property tax altogether. The corporation must notify the Commissioner within 30 days of any change in ownership/use affecting the exemption (s.5(2)(c)).
Practical note Non-corporate owners (individuals, partnerships) do not get the s.25 set-off/exemption against profits tax in the same way — they may instead elect Personal Assessment (IRO s.41) to have property tax effectively absorbed into a single personal-assessment computation where advantageous. See the Salaries Tax page for Personal Assessment.

1. What Personal Assessment is IRO s.41

Hong Kong levies direct tax under three separate heads — Salaries Tax, Profits Tax and Property Tax. Personal Assessment (PA) is not a fourth head of charge. It is a relief: an individual may elect to have all three sources aggregated into one computation, taxed at salaries-tax progressive rates after personal allowances, instead of being taxed separately at the standard rate on each source.

The one-line test for whether PA can possibly help Property Tax and Profits Tax are charged at the standard rate with no personal allowances. Salaries Tax already gets progressive rates and allowances. So PA can only help someone with property income or unincorporated-business profits. A taxpayer whose only income is employment income cannot benefit from electing — there is nothing for the election to unlock.

The election is annual, must be made in writing, and is not automatic. IRD will not apply it for you, though where an election has been made and turns out to be disadvantageous, the assessor will raise the separate assessments instead and note on the demand that PA was not to your benefit.

Distinguish from Joint Assessment Joint assessment (s.10(2)) is a Salaries Tax mechanism for married couples — it pools employment income only. Personal Assessment (s.41) pools all three heads of charge. They are different elections under different sections, and the exam trap is treating them as the same thing. See Salaries Tax §6.

Deceased taxpayers. Where a person who was eligible to elect dies, the executor has the same right of election over the deceased's total income as the deceased would have had.

3. Married couples IRO s.41(1A), s.42(2)

Separate election has been available since YA 2018/19 The Inland Revenue (Amendment) (No. 9) Ordinance 2018 relaxed what had been a hard joint-election requirement. From YA 2018/19, a married person may elect for Personal Assessment separately from, or jointly with, their spouse.
SituationWho elects
2018/19 onwards, general ruleEither spouse may elect on their own. A joint election remains available where either or both are eligible and both have income assessable under the Ordinance.
Already jointly assessed under Salaries TaxThe PA election must be made jointly. A couple who elected joint assessment under s.10(2) cannot then split for PA.
Up to 2017/18Where both spouses had assessable income and were not living apart, the election had to be made by both. If one refused, neither could elect.

Where a joint election is made, each spouse's total income as appropriately reduced is aggregated into a joint reduced total income, the married person's allowance and other allowances are deducted from the joint figure, and the tax is computed once. The total tax is then apportioned between the spouses in the ratio of their respective reduced total incomes, and each receives their own notice of assessment and demand for payment.

Why the 2018/19 change matters in practice Under the old joint-election rule, electing meant each spouse necessarily learned the other's income and business losses. A spouse who wanted to keep that private could simply refuse, and the couple lost the relief entirely. Separate election removes that veto.

4. Time limits — election and withdrawal IRO s.41(2), s.41(3)

ActionDeadline
Election The later of:
  • 2 years after the end of the year of assessment concerned — so for YA 2026/27, not later than 31 March 2029; or
  • 2 months after the issue of a notice of assessment or additional assessment for that year.
Withdrawal Within 6 months of the date the Personal Assessment is issued, or such longer period as the Commissioner considers reasonable. Where the election was made jointly, the withdrawal must also be joint.

An election is made in writing — in practice by completing the relevant section of the Tax Return (BIR60), or on Form IR76C.

Withdrawal is close to one-way Once an election is withdrawn, the taxpayer may not elect again for that same year unless the Commissioner considers a re-election appropriate. Because the two-year election window is generous but the six-month withdrawal window is short, the safer sequence is to compute both outcomes before electing rather than electing speculatively and unwinding it later.

5. The computation, step by step IRO s.42, s.42A, s.43

Three steps: aggregate, deduct, then compare against the standard rate.

StepWhat happens
1Aggregate income chargeable to Property Tax, Salaries Tax and Profits Tax into total income.
2Deduct, in order: interest on money borrowed to produce property income → concessionary deductions → business losses of the year → PA losses brought forward. This gives reduced total income. Then deduct personal allowances to give net chargeable income.
3Compute tax as the lower of: progressive rates on net chargeable income, or the standard rate on reduced total income.
The standard-rate cap applies to PA too — and on the right base The cap compares progressive tax on net chargeable income (after allowances) against standard rate on reduced total income (before allowances). Applying the standard rate to the post-allowance figure is a classic error: it uses the wrong base and understates the cap.

7. When it helps — and when it does not

PA is likely to help where

  • Property is mortgaged — Property Tax gives no relief at all for loan interest.
  • There is a business loss to set against salary or property income in the same year.
  • Total income is low enough that the progressive rates average out below the standard rate.
  • Personal allowances are unused because the income is property or business income rather than salary.
  • Concessionary deductions (donations, elderly care) are being wasted against income that gets no relief for them.

PA is unlikely to help where

  • The only income is employment income — Salaries Tax already applies progressive rates and allowances, so there is nothing to gain.
  • Total income is high: the 17% marginal rate exceeds the 15% standard rate, so aggregating drags property or business income up to 17%.
  • The property is unmortgaged and allowances are already fully absorbed elsewhere.

Because the marginal progressive rate (17%) is above the standard rate (15%), PA is a relief that reverses into a cost once income is large enough. It must be computed both ways, not assumed.

9. Common pitfalls

TrapWhat is actually right
Treating PA as a fourth head of chargeIt is a relief and an election. It changes how existing chargeable income is taxed; it never makes non-chargeable income chargeable.
Assuming PA always saves taxAbove roughly the point where the 17% marginal rate bites, it costs money — Illustration 3. Always compute both ways.
Recommending PA to a salary-only taxpayerThere is nothing to gain: progressive rates and allowances already apply.
Applying the standard-rate cap to net chargeable incomeThe cap is the standard rate on reduced total income, before personal allowances.
Deducting mortgage interest above the property's NAVCapped at that property's NAV. The excess lapses; it cannot create a loss.
Bringing in an offshore business lossOffshore profits were never chargeable, so the loss is not available.
Citing the mandatory joint electionOut of date. Separate election has been available since YA 2018/19. It remains mandatory to elect jointly only where the couple is already jointly assessed for Salaries Tax.
Relying on a spouse's residence to qualifyOnly for years up to 2017/18. From 2018/19 the elector must satisfy the residence test personally.
Apportioning joint tax by gross incomeThe ratio is each spouse's reduced total income.
Missing the withdrawal windowElection runs to 2 years after the year end; withdrawal is only 6 months from the assessment, and re-election afterwards needs the Commissioner's agreement.

Stamp Duty Ordinance (Cap. 117) — a separate ordinance from the Inland Revenue Ordinance. Charged on share transfers, immovable property transactions, and leases.

1. Overview

Stamp duty is a transaction tax, not an income tax — it is charged on specific categories of instruments (documents), not on profit. The three main heads for a Hong Kong company are: (1) Hong Kong stock transfers, (2) immovable property conveyances/agreements, and (3) leases. Hong Kong has no capital gains tax, estate duty, gift tax, or VAT — stamp duty is the main transaction-based levy filling that gap.

2026 amendment Stamp Duty (Amendment) Ordinance 2026 (gazetted 29 May 2026, effective retrospectively from 26 February 2026) revised the residential/non-residential AVD rate bands — figures below reflect the post-26-Feb-2026 rates per PwC (reviewed 22 Jul 2026). Verify against the gazetted schedule before relying on a specific banding for a transaction.

2. Share Transfers SDO s.19 (contract notes), First Schedule Head 2(1)

Ad valorem duty on transfer of Hong Kong stock0.2% of consideration or market value, whichever is higher (split 0.1% buyer / 0.1% seller, plus a small fixed contract note duty)

Contract notes are charged under s.19 (First Schedule Head 2(1)); s.21 prohibits stock being passed through without duty on the intervening step, s.22 covers consideration paid in stock/securities, and s.23 covers periodical-payment consideration. s.20 (formerly a related contract-note provision) has been repealed (33 of 2024 s.5) — do not cite it. Reliefs exist for stock borrowing/lending transactions (s.19(11) excludes a stock borrowing/return from the charge; s.19(12A) sets the borrower's return conditions; registration fee currently $270, must register within 30 days) and for intra-group ("associated companies") transfers under s.45 (§6 covers this in more detail) — see SOIPN 2.

New — RMB settlement of stamp duty on dual-counter stock The Stamp Duty (Amendment) (No. 2) Ordinance 2026 (Bill passed 8 July 2026, gazetted 17 July 2026) lets stamp duty on a transfer of dual-counter stock traded at the RMB counter be calculated and paid in Renminbi, so an investor settles the trade and the duty in the same currency at the same counter. The 0.2% rate is unchanged — this is a settlement-currency and administration change only. Commencement is on a day to be appointed by the Secretary for Financial Services and the Treasury, pending HKEX system readiness, so it is not yet in force.
Source: Government welcomes passage of Stamp Duty (Amendment) (No. 2) Bill 2026, 8 Jul 2026.

2A. Conveyances & Agreements for Sale — Charging Mechanics SDO s.4, Part IIIA (ss.29A–29DV)

The general charge is s.4 ("Charging of, liability for, and recovery of stamp duty") read together with the First Schedule, which sets out each "Head of charge" and its rate. For immovable property, Part IIIA (originally ss.29A–29I, now expanded to ss.29A–29DV across 7 Divisions covering interpretation, conveyances, agreements for sale, refunds, and the AVD scale machinery) supplies the detailed mechanics — including the provisional/formal agreement stamping rules illustrated in DIPN/SOIPN 1 below.

3. Property Transfers — Ad Valorem Duty (AVD) First Schedule Head 1(1)/(1A); ss.29AI, 29AJ, 29AK

Correction vs. common assumption AVD Scale 1 is not a flat 15% on residential property. The flat-15% Scale 1 that applied from 5 Nov 2016 was superseded by a 2023 reform that made Scale 1 a graduated table matching Scale 2's bands (top rate 4.25%) — the only remaining difference is a new top band the Stamp Duty (Amendment) Ordinance 2026 added above ~$109.57M (see below). If your prior understanding was "flat 15% for non-HKPR/company buyers," that is now out of date except at the very top of the market.

6. Reliefs SDO s.45; s.29AD

Associated bodies corporate relief (s.45) — relieves a conveyance from one associated body corporate to another from AVD, SSD, BSD, and HK-stock contract-note duty (it spans Heads 1(1), 1(1AA), 1(1AAB), 2(1) and 2(3)), where a ≥90% common-ownership test is met. A clawback applies under s.45(5A) if the ≥90% association ceases within 2 years of the relieved conveyance.

Closely related persons (s.29AD) — defines the parent/spouse/child/sibling relationships that qualify for the Scale 2 AVD concession (§3) and are also picked up by the close-relative exemption notes applied to the BSD/SSD sub-heads (co-purchasers who are closely related are treated as "the same person" for ownership-counting purposes).

Proposed — newborn-family stamp duty concession, 2026 Policy Address (16 Sep 2026) A family with a child born in Hong Kong on or after 16 September 2026, where at least one parent is a Hong Kong permanent resident, would get a stamp duty waiver capped at HKD 20,000 on a residential property purchased in the window running from one year before to two years after the birth. To take effect on enactment of the amendment ordinance — not yet law, so do not apply it to a current transaction. Note it is a capped waiver, not a rate change: on any purchase where AVD exceeds HKD 20,000 the relief is a flat HKD 20,000.
Source: IRD — 2026 Policy Address, Tax-related Policy Measures (on Individuals), published 16 Sep 2026.

7. Partnership Admission & Retirement

Where a partner is admitted to, or retires from, a partnership that holds Hong Kong stock or immovable property, the change in each partner's proportionate interest can itself be a chargeable "sale and purchase" of the underlying property/stock for stamp duty purposes (the incoming/continuing partners are treated as acquiring the outgoing/diluted partners' fractional interest). This is assessed using the same AVD/stock-transfer rules above, applied to the fractional interest that changes hands — always check partnership deeds and capital-account movements for a hidden stamp duty trigger whenever partners change.

Depreciation & Allowances

Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), Part 6 & related provisions — capital allowances replace accounting depreciation for tax purposes; accounting depreciation itself is never deductible (IRO s.17(1)(c)).

折舊及免稅額整理工業/商業建築物免稅額、機械及設備、初期/每年免稅額、結餘課稅/免稅額及特定資產即時扣除。

1. Overview

Because capital expenditure and accounting depreciation are non-deductible under Profits Tax (s.17(1)(c)/(d)), Part 6 of the IRO substitutes a separate, statutory system of capital allowances for four categories of qualifying capital expenditure: (1) industrial buildings, (2) commercial buildings, (3) plant & machinery, and (4) a growing list of 100%/5-year write-off regimes for specific asset types (ss.16C–16L) that sit alongside, and interact with, Part 6.

Key mechanic — add-back and allowance in the same computation In every profits tax computation: add back accounting depreciation (non-deductible), then deduct the correct statutory allowance instead. The two numbers are almost never equal — this is the single most common adjustment in a Hong Kong tax computation.

2. Industrial & Commercial Buildings IRO ss.33A, 34, 36, 37

Building typeInitial allowanceAnnual allowanceBalancing allowance/charge
Industrial building or structure (mills, factories, docks, utility undertakings, qualifying storage/farming/R&D buildings)20% (1/5) of cost, year of first use4% (1/25) straight-line on costOn sale/lease termination/demolition — balancing charge capped at total allowances previously granted
Commercial building or structure (all other non-excluded buildings)None4% (1/25) straight-line on cost, from 1998/99Same balancing mechanism as industrial buildings, from 1998/99

"Industrial building or structure" is narrowly defined (DIPN 2) — it excludes dwellings, retail shops, showrooms, hotels, and offices. Where a building is used partly for a qualifying industrial purpose and partly not, a 10% de-minimis rule applies: if the non-qualifying use is no more than 10% of the whole, the building is treated as wholly industrial. On a sale, the buyer's future annual allowance is recalculated as the residue of expenditure after sale, spread over the remaining years to the building's 25th year from first use.

Historical note Before 1998/99, commercial buildings only qualified for a much smaller "rebuilding allowance" (0.75% pre-1990/91, then 2% from 1990/91–1997/98) rather than the modern 4% annual allowance — relevant only when tracing the residue of expenditure on a very old building still being written down today.

IRD Administration

Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) — filing, assessment, objections, appeals, provisional tax and penalty mechanics that apply across Profits, Property and Salaries Tax.

1. Overview

These provisions are common infrastructure across Profits Tax, Property Tax and Salaries Tax — a computation is only complete once you know how it gets filed, assessed, and (if needed) disputed or paid provisionally.

2. Returns & Filing IRO s.51

The Commissioner issues annual returns (profits tax, salaries tax, property tax, employer's return) which must be completed and filed by the stated due date — extensions are routinely available for corporate profits tax returns filed through a tax representative under the Block Extension Scheme. Every person carrying on a trade, profession or business must keep sufficient records (s.51C) for at least 7 years to enable profits/losses to be readily ascertained.

Practical note For a newly incorporated company, the first profits tax return is usually issued around 18 months after incorporation or commencement of business — but a company must still notify the Commissioner of chargeability (s.51(2)) within 4 months after the end of the basis period if no return has been issued, even where no return has yet been received.
Block Extension Scheme — 2025/26 returns, "D" code extended By circular letter of 14 July 2026, the Commissioner further extended the due date for 2025/26 profits tax returns with Accounting Date Code "D" (accounting date falling between 1 and 31 December 2025):
  • Paper filing: 17 August 2026 → 31 August 2026
  • Electronic filing: 17 September 2026 → 2 October 2026
The extension was granted to help businesses and practitioners adapt to new compliance requirements; the circular still urges representatives to file well before the extended date. Codes "N" and "M" were not changed by this circular. Source: Circular Letter to Tax Representatives — Block Extension Scheme for Lodgement of 2025/26 Tax Returns, 14 Jul 2026.
Business Tax Portal — new Responsible Person capacities (8 Jun 2026) IRD expanded the capacities in which a Responsible Person may register a business for the Business Tax Portal (BTP). Check the registration capacity before opening a BTP account for a client entity — an account opened in the wrong capacity has to be redone.

3. Assessment & Estimated Assessment IRO ss.59–60

The assessor assesses the return as filed, or — where no return is filed, or the assessor is not satisfied with a return — issues an estimated assessment based on the best information available. Estimated assessments are commonly issued precisely to protect the statutory time limit for assessment where a return is outstanding; they are not a final determination and are superseded once the actual figures are agreed (usually via an objection, §4).

Additional assessments may be raised within the statutory time limit (generally 6 years from the end of the relevant year of assessment, extended to 10 years in cases involving fraud or wilful default) where profits/income have been understated or under-assessed.

4. Objections & Appeals IRO s.64 (objections), s.66 (Board of Review), s.69 (case stated)

StageTime limitForum
Notice of objection1 month from the date of the assessmentCommissioner of Inland Revenue
Appeal against the Commissioner's determination1 month from the determinationBoard of Review
Case stated appeal on a point of law1 month from the Board's decisionCourt of First Instance, then Court of Appeal / Court of Final Appeal

An objection must state precisely the grounds of objection — a bare, unparticularised objection can be rejected as invalid. Tax under dispute is not automatically suspended; the taxpayer must separately apply to hold over payment of the tax in dispute (or the portion in dispute) pending resolution.

5. Provisional Tax & Holdover IRO ss.63A–63U, 71

Provisional profits tax, provisional salaries tax, and provisional property tax are each charged in advance, generally based on the immediately preceding year's assessable profits/income, and later set off against the final assessment for that year once it issues. A taxpayer may apply to hold over payment of the whole or part of provisional tax where, for example, the current year's profits/income are objectively likely to be significantly lower than the prior year's, or personal allowances have increased, or an objection is pending on the immediately preceding year's assessment.

Practical note Holdover applications have their own strict statutory deadlines (broadly, the later of 28 days before the provisional tax is due, or 14 days after the provisional tax notice is issued) — missing the window forfeits the holdover regardless of the merits.

6. Penalties IRO ss.80, 82, 82A

ContraventionConsequence
Failure to file a return, or filing late, without reasonable excuseSummary conviction fine and/or additional tax (s.80)
Making an incorrect return, or omitting income, without reasonable excuseAdditional tax up to treble the tax undercharged (s.82A) — an administrative penalty assessed by the Commissioner, distinct from criminal prosecution
Wilfully with intent to evade, or assisting another to evade, taxCriminal prosecution (s.82) — fine up to treble the tax undercharged plus imprisonment up to 3 years

Additional tax under s.82A is an administrative sanction (no need to prove criminal intent) and can be settled by way of a compound penalty offer from the Commissioner in less serious cases — this is the mechanism most commonly encountered in routine tax computation corrections and voluntary disclosures.

7. Double Taxation Relief & Tax Clearance IRO ss.49, 50, 51D

Where Hong Kong has a comprehensive double taxation agreement (CDTA) with another jurisdiction, s.50 provides a tax credit for foreign tax paid on income also taxed in Hong Kong (interacting with the s.16(1)(c)/(2J) deduction restriction on the Profits Tax page). Employers must comply with tax clearance obligations (s.51D) before an employee who is about to leave Hong Kong permanently, or for a substantial period, ceases employment — including withholding final payments until IRD confirms clearance or a specified period has elapsed.

CDTA network (as at 24 September 2026): Hong Kong signed its 60th comprehensive double taxation agreement with Slovenia on 4 September 2026, following Nigeria (13 July 2026) and Cyprus (12 June 2026). Each enters into force only after both sides complete ratification — check the IRD CDTA list for the in-force date before claiming treaty relief.

  • CARF — bill stage. 9. BIR52 — How It Differs from BIR51

Structurally parallel but shorter (129 boxes → ~111) and reorganized. The headline differences:

  • Part 1 extras (boxes 4–5): whether the proprietor/spouse/partner received emoluments or interest on capital from the business — such payments are non-deductible under s.17(2), and box 5 confirms the tax computation adjusts for it. The two-tiered rate election is box 6 here, not box 4.
  • Part 6 is entirely new: "Personal Particulars of Proprietor or Partners and Allocation of Profits/Loss and Claim for Deduction of Mandatory Contributions" (boxes 18–37) — per-partner HKID/BR number, profit/loss sharing ratio, a Personal Assessment election flag (the actual election is made on the individual's own BIR60, not here), allocated Assessable Profits/Adjusted Loss, and MPF contributions claimed. The allocation total must equal Part 1's box 1/2. See the Personal Assessment section.
  • Part 7 drops the corporation-only items (RBC regime, Companies Ordinance amalgamation, private-company/shareholder-change questions) and adds business-specific ones: cessation by death of the proprietor, and property purchase for building allowance purposes.
  • Part 9 lists only 10 supplementary forms (the corporation-only concessions S6–S14, S16–S18 don't apply).
  • Part 10 swaps BIR51's "interest to non-HK associated corporations" box for a "mandatory MPF contributions for proprietor/partners" deduction.
  • No separate depreciation-allowances Part — Financial Data shifts one part earlier, and the corporation-only "share-based payments"/"issued share capital" fields are dropped.
  • Declaration title varies by entity type: sole proprietorship → "person"; partnership → "precedent partner"; limited partnership fund → "general partner" / "investment manager"; body of persons → "principal officer"; no resident proprietor/partner → "agent"/"manager"; deceased estate → "executor."

10. BIR54 — What It Actually Is

Common misconception, corrected BIR54 is not a second "persons other than corporations" return. Its actual title is "Profits Tax Return — In Respect of Non-Resident Persons." It is filed under ss.20A/20B by the Hong Kong resident payer or agent, in the non-resident's own name, to report that non-resident's Hong Kong-sourced trade/business profits — most commonly royalty/IP payments, or income received via a Hong Kong agent. It's bilingual (Chinese/English) and far shorter than BIR51/52 (~22 boxes).

Key sections: a two-tiered rate election made on behalf of the non-resident (box 3, with the same connected-entity disclosure logic as BIR51/S1); an IP use/assignment block (boxes 11–17) determining whether the deemed-profit computation under s.21A(1)(a) applies 100% or 30% of the royalty receipt as assessable profit; a section on the agent's role in Hong Kong; and a reciprocal shipowner exemption block (boxes 18–20, s.23B(4A)) for non-resident shipowners.

Illustration — Lease premium: Profits Tax vs Property Tax interaction DIPN 4

Company H grants a 6-year lease of its property for a premium of $1,200,000, paid up front. As a person carrying on a property-dealing/letting business, the premium is a revenue receipt taxable under Profits Tax — s.15C spreads it evenly over the lease term, so 1/6 = $200,000 is assessed each year for 6 years. Separately, s.5B(4) deems the premium payable in equal monthly instalments for Property Tax purposes but capped at 3 years, so 1/3 = $400,000 is assessed for Property Tax in each of Years 1–3 only.

YearProperty Tax paid
($400,000 × 80% × 16%)		Profits Tax
($200,000 × 17.5%)		Net position
1–3 (each)$51,200$35,000Refund of $16,200 (Property Tax set off against Profits Tax under s.25)
4–6 (each)—$35,000$35,000 payable

Grand total tax over 6 years = $210,000, i.e. exactly 17.5% of the $1,200,000 premium (the historical standard rate used in the DIPN's example year) — the set-off mechanism ensures the premium is not taxed twice under both heads.

Illustration — Interest deduction restricted by a tax-free secured deposit DIPN 13A

Taxpayer F borrows $1,000,000 at 5% interest, secured by a $1,000,000 deposit earning tax-exempt interest at 4%. Because the loan is secured on a deposit generating tax-free income, s.16(2A)/(2B) restrict the deduction: the $40,000 tax-free interest earned reduces the otherwise-deductible $50,000 loan interest, leaving only $10,000 deductible.

Illustration — Service company "Type II" management fee cap DIPN 24

A professional firm pays $4,000,000 in management fees to a related service company whose actual attributable cost of providing those services is $3,000,000. The deduction is capped at cost plus a 12.5% mark-up: $3,000,000 × 112.5% = $3,375,000. The $625,000 excess is added back, increasing assessable profits from $1,000,000 (per accounts) to $1,625,000.

Illustration — Foreign tax deductible only where no DTA credit is available DIPN 28

Bank-HK pays income tax on interest income in Jurisdiction F. If Hong Kong has no double tax agreement with Jurisdiction F, the foreign tax is deductible under s.16(1)(c). If Hong Kong does have a DTA with Jurisdiction F, s.16(2J) denies the deduction — Bank-HK must instead claim a tax credit under s.50, since double relief (deduction plus credit) is not permitted.

Illustration — Hong Kong bank deposit interest exemption straddling the exemption date DIPN 34

A taxpayer has $10,000,000 trading profits plus $4,000,000 of local bank deposit interest, of which $1,000,000 accrued before 22 June 1998 (the exemption's effective date) and $3,000,000 accrued after. Only the pre-exemption $1,000,000 is taxable; total 1998/99 assessable profits = $11,000,000 rather than $14,000,000.

Illustration — Prepaid expenses follow accounting treatment, not cash timing DIPN 40, following CIR v Secan

Following the Secan case, a full cash prepayment (e.g. 3 years' rent or insurance paid up front) is no longer deductible in full in the year of payment if the applicable accounting standard requires it to be amortised over the period it relates to — the tax deduction now follows the accounting/matching treatment, reversing the Department's older cash-basis practice.

Illustration — Basis period on commencement: first accounts cover 18 months Module 9, Ch.3 §3.9.2 — s.18C(1)(b)

A company commences business on 1 July 2018 and adopts a 31 December accounting date, but its first set of accounts is not closed until 31 December 2019 — an 18-month period. Adjusted profits: $90,000 for the 18 months to 31 Dec 2019, then $68,000 for the 12 months to 31 Dec 2020.

Where the first accounting period exceeds 12 months, s.18C(1)(b) gives the Commissioner discretion to take the second year of assessment as the 12 months counted back from the end of the first accounting period, with the commencement year taking the remaining stub — both by time-apportioning the 18-month profit.

Year of assessmentBasis periodAssessable profitsBasis
Commencement year1/7/2018 – 31/12/2018 (6 mo.)$30,000 (= $90,000 × 6/18)s.18C(1)(b)
Following year1/1/2019 – 31/12/2019 (12 mo.)$60,000 (= $90,000 × 12/18)s.18B(2)

Check: $30,000 + $60,000 = $90,000 — the full 18-month profit is accounted for exactly once. No tax rate is involved in this mechanic, so no currency correction was needed.

Illustration — Retirement scheme contribution cap (s.17(1)(h)) Module 9, Ch.3 §3.5.4

An employee earns an annual salary of $100,000. The employer makes an ordinary annual contribution to a recognised retirement scheme equal to 20% of salary, i.e. $20,000.

s.17(1)(h) disallows the deduction of ordinary retirement-scheme contributions to the extent they exceed 15% of the employee's total emoluments: cap = 15% × $100,000 = $15,000 deductible; the remaining $5,000 is non-deductible.

The 15% cap is unchanged in current law — no correction needed.

Illustration — Interest flow-back test: debenture interest paid to an untaxed associate Module 9, Ch.3 §3.5.3.8 — s.16(2C), adapted from DIPN 13A

A Hong Kong company issues $10,000,000 of debentures to fund its HK trading business, paying $1,000,000 total interest for the year. $8,000,000 of the debentures is held all year by its overseas subsidiary, an associate not taxed in Hong Kong on the interest it receives.

Proportion held by the untaxed associate$8,000,000 / $10,000,000 = 8/10

Exception: no restriction applies if the associate is a market maker holding the debentures in the ordinary course of its business (s.16(2G)). No profits tax rate is involved, so no correction was needed.

Illustration — Joint assessment saves tax vs separate taxation DIPN 18

Mr A earns $680,000, Mrs A earns $100,000. Taxed separately, their combined liability is $23,030. Electing joint assessment under s.10(2) — pooling income and allowances — reduces the combined liability to $17,505, because Mrs A's unabsorbed allowances/deductions offset Mr A's higher-rate income.

Illustration — Home loan interest apportionment by ownership type DIPN 35

Mr C is sole owner, pays $120,000 interest — allowable deduction capped at $100,000. Mr & Mrs D are joint tenants, pay $100,000 total interest between them — each gets $50,000. Mr E (1/4 share) and Mr F (3/4 share) are tenants in common on a $100,000 interest bill — Mr E gets $25,000, Mr F gets $75,000 (apportioned strictly by ownership share, not by who actually paid).

Illustration — VHIS premium cap applied per insured person DIPN 56

Mr J pays VHIS premiums for himself ($5,000), spouse ($4,000), grandmother ($16,000), father ($12,000), mother ($11,000), son ($3,000) — total $51,000 paid. Because the cap is HKD 8,000 per insured person, the grandmother/father/mother claims are each capped at $8,000: allowable deduction = $5,000 + $4,000 + ($8,000 × 3) + $3,000 = $36,000. The $15,000 excess simply lapses — no carry-forward.

Illustration — Charitable donation cap at 35% of reduced assessable income DIPN 37

Mr A has salary $500,000, deductible expenses $10,000 (reduced assessable income $490,000), and made donations of $210,000. Since $210,000 exceeds 35% of $490,000, the deduction is capped at $490,000 × 35% = $171,500 — the remaining $38,500 of donations gives no further tax benefit.

Illustration — Share option gain apportioned by vesting days in Hong Kong DIPN 38

An employee's share option has a 730-day vesting period, of which 292 days were spent working in Hong Kong. At exercise, only 292/730 = 40% of the gain is assessable to Hong Kong salaries tax — the remainder relates to non-Hong Kong employment and falls outside the charge.

Illustration — The 60-day rule: two early departures tip the count over the line Module 9, Ch.4 §4.1 — s.8(1B)

An employee holds a Hong Kong employment contract but works full-time in Mainland China, returning to Hong Kong only periodically. His visits (quarterly meetings plus connecting travel) total 59 "ordinary" days for the year. On two occasions, however, he leaves Hong Kong very early in the morning to connect onward (once via Osaka, once via London) rather than flying straight back to China. Under the counting convention, both the day of arrival and the day of departure count as a full day each — even for a same-day transit.

Days in HK from ordinary visits59
Extra day — early departure via Osaka+1
Extra day — early departure via London+1
Total days in Hong Kong61

61 > 60, so the s.8(1B) exemption fails entirely — his full Hong Kong-employment income becomes chargeable, even though he worked in China almost all year. It's a hard cliff-edge test on days present, not days worked, and transit days count in full. No allowance/rate figures involved, so no correction was needed.

An employee on a $600,000 salary gets a company-leased apartment rent-free; the employer also reimburses $16,800 of medical insurance (a taxable perquisite). Rental value = 10% of (salary + taxable perquisites) for a self-contained flat/house.

Illustration PT-1 Badges of trade

Question. A company buys a shop unit with short-term bank finance, renovates it lightly, advertises it for resale within three months, and sells at a gain. Is the gain likely capital or trading?

中文題目：一間公司以短期銀行貸款購入商舖，簡單裝修後三個月內放售並賺取收益。該收益較可能屬資本性質，還是營業買賣收益？

Question. A retailer buys a warehouse for storage, uses it for several years, then sells it after receiving an unsolicited offer. Is the gain automatically taxable because the seller is a business?

中文答案：一般在期權被行使、放棄或轉讓時，按相關事件計算應課稅利益，通常會參考市值與行使價等規則計算收益。

3. Property Tax - Rental Income and Corporate Owners

Illustration PrT-1 Tenant-paid expenses

Question. A tenant pays HK$20,000 repair costs that the landlord was obliged to bear under the lease. Is the payment ignored for property tax?

中文題目：租客支付 HK$20,000 修葺費，而根據租約該費用本應由業主承擔。物業稅是否可忽略該款項？

Answer. No. Where the tenant discharges an obligation of the owner, the amount can be treated as consideration for use of the property. The statutory 20% allowance then applies in computing net assessable value.

中文答案：不可忽略。若租客代業主履行責任，該款項可被視為使用物業的代價，然後在計算應評稅淨值時扣除 20% 法定修葺免稅額。

Illustration PrT-2 Simple NAV computation

Question. Annual rent is HK$360,000. Rates of HK$12,000 are paid by the owner. No irrecoverable rent. Compute net assessable value.

中文題目：全年租金 HK$360,000，差餉 HK$12,000 由業主支付，沒有不能追回租金。計算應評稅淨值。

Assessable value before allowance: 360,000 - 12,000 = 348,000
Less statutory repairs allowance: 348,000 x 20% = 69,600
Net assessable value: 278,400

Answer. NAV is HK$278,400.

中文答案：應評稅淨值為 HK$278,400。

Question PrT-3 Corporate property owner

Question. A Hong Kong company lets out an office and the rental income is already included in profits tax. Can it avoid double tax under property tax?

中文題目：香港公司出租辦公室，租金收入已納入利得稅課稅。可否避免再繳物業稅？

Answer. A corporation can usually seek exemption from property tax where the property income is chargeable to profits tax. The rental stream is then dealt with under profits tax rather than being taxed twice.

中文答案：公司如相關物業收入已須繳利得稅，通常可申請豁免物業稅，避免同一租金收入被重複課稅。

4. Personal Assessment - Election and Loss Set-off

Illustration PA-1 Mortgage interest limit

Question. Under personal assessment, an individual has property NAV of HK$60,000 and interest of HK$75,000 on the loan used to buy that income-producing property. How much interest can be deducted against that property income?

中文題目：在個人入息課稅下，某人有物業應評稅淨值 HK$60,000，購買該收租物業的貸款利息為 HK$75,000。可扣除多少利息？

Answer. HK$60,000. The property-loan interest deduction is limited by the net assessable value of the relevant property included in personal assessment.

中文答案：HK$60,000。可扣除的物業貸款利息以納入個人入息課稅的相關物業應評稅淨值為上限。

Illustration PA-2 Business loss limit

Question. Total income after allowable deductions becomes negative because of concessionary deductions and a business loss. How much personal assessment loss can be carried forward?

中文題目：扣除特惠扣除及業務虧損後，總入息變成負數。可結轉的個人入息課稅虧損是多少？

Answer. The carry-forward personal assessment loss is limited to the business loss element. A deficit caused by personal allowances or non-business concessionary deductions is not carried forward as a business loss.

中文答案：可結轉的個人入息課稅虧損以業務虧損部分為限。由個人免稅額或非業務性特惠扣除造成的不足額，不會作為業務虧損結轉。

Question PA-3 Married couple election

Question. A married person wants personal assessment, but the spouse also has income chargeable to tax. Can one spouse elect alone?

中文題目：已婚人士想選擇個人入息課稅，但配偶亦有應課稅收入。一方可否單獨選擇？

Answer. Generally no. Where both spouses have income within the personal assessment framework, joint election is required. This is important when modelling whether personal assessment is beneficial.

中文答案：一般不可以。若夫婦雙方均有屬個人入息課稅框架內的收入，通常需要共同選擇。判斷個人入息課稅是否有利時必須留意這點。

5. Stamp Duty - Instruments, Property, Shares, Relief

Question SD-1 Instrument-based tax

Question. Why is it imprecise to say that stamp duty taxes "transactions"?

中文題目：為何說印花稅是對「交易」課稅並不精確？

Answer. Stamp duty is charged on instruments. A transaction matters because it produces or explains the instrument, but the charge is imposed by reference to the stampable document and the relevant charging head.

中文答案：印花稅是對文書課稅。交易本身重要，是因為它產生或解釋該文書；但真正課稅基礎是可予徵收印花稅的文件及相關課稅項目。

Illustration SD-2 Hong Kong stock transfer

Question. Hong Kong shares are sold for HK$100,000 but market value is HK$120,000. Which amount is used for ad valorem stamp duty?

中文題目：香港股票以 HK$100,000 出售，但市值為 HK$120,000。從價印花稅應以哪個金額計算？

Answer. HK$120,000. For Hong Kong stock transfers, duty is generally computed on the higher of consideration and value. Check the current stock duty rate before filing, as rates have changed over time.

中文答案：HK$120,000。香港股票轉讓一般按代價及價值兩者中較高者計算印花稅。由於股票印花稅率曾有變動，實際申報前應查核現行稅率。

Question SD-3 Associated company relief

Question. ParentCo transfers Hong Kong immovable property to a 100%-owned subsidiary. What relief may be relevant?

中文題目：母公司把香港不動產轉讓予其 100% 擁有的附屬公司。可能涉及哪項印花稅寬免？

Answer. Associated-company relief under SDO s.45 may be relevant if the ownership threshold and continuity conditions are satisfied. The parties must monitor the post-transfer association period because relief can be clawed back if the conditions break.

中文答案：如符合持股比例及持續關聯條件，可能適用《印花稅條例》第 45 條關聯公司寬免。轉讓後仍須監察關聯關係維持期，否則寬免可能被撤回。

6. Cross-border Profits Tax - Withholding and Non-residents

Question CB-1 Non-resident entertainer

Question. A Hong Kong event organizer pays a non-resident performer's company for a Hong Kong performance. Why might Hong Kong tax be withheld even if the performer is paid through a company?

中文題目：香港活動主辦方向非居民表演者的公司支付香港演出費。即使款項經公司支付，為何仍可能需要在香港預扣稅？

Answer. The IRO contains deeming and collection rules for non-resident entertainers and sportspeople. Interposing a company does not necessarily remove the Hong Kong charge where the performance activity is in Hong Kong.

中文答案：《稅務條例》對非居民演藝人員及運動員有視作收入及徵收規則。即使中間有公司收款，只要演出活動在香港進行，未必能避免香港稅務責任。

Illustration CB-2 Deemed profit method

Question. If the law taxes a non-resident entertainer on a deemed profit basis, what are the two moving parts in the computation?

中文題目：若法例以視作利潤方法向非居民表演者課稅，計算時主要有哪兩個變動部分？

Answer. First identify the gross payment that falls within the deeming rule. Then apply the statutory deemed-profit fraction and the applicable tax rate. If the actual-profit method is claimed, only properly connected deductible expenses should be used.

中文答案：第一，確定受視作規則涵蓋的總付款；第二，套用法定視作利潤比例及適用稅率。如改用實際利潤方法，只應扣除與香港演出適當相關的可扣稅開支。
