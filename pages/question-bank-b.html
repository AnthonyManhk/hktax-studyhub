Case 1 — Ms Fiona Ng (10 marks)

Fiona Ng is employed as marketing manager by Silverline Retail Ltd, a company centrally managed and controlled in Hong Kong. Her annual salary for the year of assessment 2026/27 is $960,000. Silverline provides her with a self-contained flat, rent free, for the whole year; Fiona has no outgoings deductible under s.12(1)(a) against her salary.

Separately, Fiona wholly owns a second residential flat, which she lets out (unconnected with her employer) for the whole year at $18,000 a month. She personally paid rates of $6,500 for the year on that flat. She is single and claims only the basic allowance ($145,000 for 2026/27); she has no mortgage on either property and no other income, deductions or losses.
Rental value

B001. What rental value must be included in Fiona's assessable income for the flat Silverline provides rent free?

Correct: A — $96,000.

The flat is self-contained accommodation, so the rate is 10% of income after s.12(1)(a) outgoings. With no outgoings to deduct, that base is her full salary: $960,000 × 10% = $96,000.

s.9(2)
Assessable income

B002. What is Fiona's total assessable income from her employment for the year (salary plus the rental value of the flat)?

Correct: B — $1,056,000.

$960,000 salary + $96,000 rental value = $1,056,000. Omitting the rental value entirely, or computing it at the wrong percentage (8% applies to two hotel rooms, not a self-contained flat), are the two errors this question is designed to catch.

s.9(1)(a), s.9(2)
Property tax

B003. What is the property tax payable on Fiona's own let flat for the year?

Correct: C — $25,140.

Assessable value $216,000 ($18,000 × 12); less rates paid by the owner $6,500 = $209,500; less the 20% statutory allowance $41,900 = net assessable value $167,600; × 15% = $25,140. Forgetting the rates deduction, forgetting the 20% allowance, or forgetting both and taxing the gross rent, each give one of the other three options.

ss.5, 5(1A)
Salaries tax computation

B004. Ignoring any election for personal assessment, what is Fiona's salaries tax payable for the year?

Correct: D — $136,870.

Progressive rates on net chargeable income ($1,056,000 − $145,000 allowance = $911,000) give $136,870; the standard rate on net income before allowances ($1,056,000) gives $158,400. Salaries tax payable is the lower of the two: $136,870. Applying the standard rate to income after allowances instead of before (giving $136,650) is a close-looking but wrong alternative route to this figure.

s.13
Personal assessment

B005. Should Fiona elect personal assessment for the year?

Correct: A — No — total tax without electing ($162,010) is lower than with personal assessment ($165,362): there is no mortgage interest or loss for the election to unlock, and her income already sits in the 17% marginal band.

Without electing: salaries tax $136,870 + property tax $25,140 = $162,010. Electing aggregates both incomes ($1,223,600) less the same one basic allowance, computed at progressive rates capped by the standard rate on reduced total income: $165,362. Aggregating drags the property income into her 17% marginal band, above the flat 15% property tax rate, and there is nothing else (no mortgage interest, no loss) for the election to unlock — so electing costs her $3,352 more, not less. Compute both ways; never assume personal assessment helps.

Case 2 — Golden Horizon Ltd (10 marks)

Golden Horizon Ltd, a Hong Kong trading company, reports an accounting profit before tax of $5,000,000 for the year ended 31 March 2027. Its accounts include depreciation of $300,000 and a fine of $20,000 for a pollution control breach, and it paid $150,000 in approved charitable donations, all charged as expenses.

During the year Golden Horizon bought qualifying plant for $500,000 (the 20% pool's written-down value brought forward was $200,000, and there were no disposals), and incurred $1,000,000 of qualifying Type B R&D expenditure, which is included in the $1,000,000 already charged as an expense in arriving at the accounting profit above. The company has no connected entities for the two-tier regime.
Depreciation allowances

B006. What is the total depreciation allowance available on the plant pool for the year?

Correct: B — $380,000.

Initial allowance: $500,000 additions × 60% = $300,000. Reduced pool: $200,000 + $500,000 − $300,000 = $400,000. Annual allowance at 20%: $80,000. Total = IA $300,000 + AA $80,000 = $380,000. Taking the IA alone, the AA alone, or computing the AA on the pool before deducting the IA, each give one of the other three options.

s.39B; Sch. 3
R&D deduction

B007. Beyond the $1,000,000 already charged as an expense in the accounts, what additional deduction is available for the qualifying Type B R&D expenditure?

Correct: C — $2,000,000.

Type B R&D is enhanced at 300% on the first $2,000,000: $1,000,000 × 3 = $3,000,000 total allowable deduction. $1,000,000 of that is already in the accounting profit, so the additional deduction still to be made in the computation is $3,000,000 − $1,000,000 = $2,000,000. Stating the total enhanced deduction ($3,000,000) as if it were the additional amount is the commonest error - it double-counts the $1,000,000 already expensed.

s.16B
Donations

B008. What is the maximum charitable donation deduction Golden Horizon may claim?

Correct: D — $150,000.

The allowable deduction is the lower of the amount paid ($150,000) and 35% of assessable profits before donations ($1,081,500), so the full $150,000 paid is deductible — well within the $1,081,500 cap. Stating the cap itself, rather than the lower of the two, is a common error.

s.16D
Assessable profit

B009. What is Golden Horizon's assessable profit for the year?

Correct: A — $2,940,000.

$5,000,000 accounting profit + $300,000 depreciation + $150,000 donations + $20,000 fine = $5,470,000. Less the additional R&D deduction $2,000,000 = $3,470,000. Less depreciation allowances $380,000 = $3,090,000 (the base for the donation cap). Less the donation deduction $150,000 = assessable profit $2,940,000. Stopping before deducting the donation, or before deducting the depreciation allowances, gives one of the other two wrong figures.

s.14, s.16, s.17
Two-tier profits tax

B010. What is Golden Horizon's profits tax payable for the year?

Correct: B — $320,100.

The two-tier rates apply to the final assessable profit of $2,940,000: ($2,000,000 × 8.25%) + ($940,000 × 16.5%) = $320,100. Applying a flat 16.5% or flat 8.25% to the whole profit, or applying the two-tier rates to the wrong (pre-donation) profit figure of $3,090,000, each give one of the other three options.

s.14AA

Case 3 — Harbour Partners (10 marks)

Harbour Partners is a Hong Kong partnership with two partners: Mr Tam, an individual, holding a 60% profit share, and Silver Bay Ltd, a corporate partner, holding the remaining 40%. The partnership's assessable profit for the year is $4,000,000, allocated in that profit-sharing ratio, with each partner's two-tier $2,000,000 band apportioned in the same ratio.

Mr Tam's own tax on his share remained unpaid more than six months after the due date - specifically 7 months overdue when he finally settled it.
Partnership allocation

B011. What is Mr Tam's share of the partnership's assessable profit?

Correct: C — $2,400,000.

$4,000,000 × 60% = $2,400,000. Using Silver Bay's 40% share instead, using the whole partnership profit unallocated, or misapplying a 50:50 split, each give one of the other three options.

s.22
Corporate partner's tax

B012. What is Silver Bay Ltd's own profits tax payable on its 40% share, applying the two-tier rates to its apportioned band?

Correct: D — $198,000.

Silver Bay's share is $1,600,000, with an apportioned first-tier band of $800,000 ($2,000,000 × 40%) at the corporate rates: ($800,000 × 8.25%) + ($800,000 × 16.5%) = $198,000. Applying a flat 16.5% or flat 8.25% to the whole share, ignoring the apportioned band, or stating Mr Tam's figure instead, each give one of the other three options.

s.14AA; DIPN 28
Individual partner's tax

B013. What is Mr Tam's own profits tax payable on his 60% share, applying the two-tier rates to his apportioned band?

Correct: A — $270,000.

Mr Tam's share is $2,400,000, with an apportioned first-tier band of $1,200,000 ($2,000,000 × 60%) at the unincorporated rates: ($1,200,000 × 7.5%) + ($1,200,000 × 15%) = $270,000. Applying a flat 15% or flat 7.5% to the whole share, or stating Silver Bay's figure instead, each give one of the other three options.

s.14AA; DIPN 28
Total partnership tax

B014. What is the total profits tax payable in respect of Harbour Partners' profit for the year?

Correct: B — $468,000.

The partnership is not itself taxed; the total is simply the sum of what each partner pays on their own apportioned share: $270,000 + $198,000 = $468,000. Applying one flat rate to the whole $4,000,000 as if it were a single corporate taxpayer (16.5%), a single unincorporated taxpayer at the flat rate, or the two-tier corporate rates without regard to Mr Tam's individual rate, each give one of the other three - and each ignores that a mixed partnership has more than one type of partner.

s.14AA; DIPN 28
Late payment surcharge

B015. Mr Tam's own tax of $270,000 on his share was paid 7 months after the due date. What is the total amount he actually had to pay, including surcharges?

Correct: C — $311,850.

A 5% surcharge arises on the due date: $270,000 × 1.05 = $283,500. Because it remained unpaid beyond 6 months, a further 10% is charged on the tax plus that first surcharge: $283,500 × 1.10 = $311,850. Stopping at the first 5% surcharge, applying a flat 10% instead of the compounding two-step charge, or applying the second 10% to the original tax rather than to tax-plus-first-surcharge, each give one of the other three options.

s.71(5), s.71(5A)
