Computation Formats

The standard pro-forma layout for each Hong Kong tax, in the order the lines belong, with what goes on each line and one worked example apiece.

Where these come from The layouts follow Hong Kong Taxation and Tax Planning (22nd ed., Ho & Mak) — Ch.7 property tax, Ch.12 salaries tax, Ch.20 profits tax, Ch.31 plant and machinery. That book states the law to 31 May 2024, so every rate and allowance has been re-checked against IRD and updated where it has moved. § What has changed lists each difference rather than silently absorbing it.

How to use these

A tax computation is marked on structure as much as on arithmetic. Getting a figure into the wrong line — or the right figure in the right line but the wrong order — loses the marks even when the final number is close. These are the skeletons to have in your head before you start writing.

ConventionMeaning
XA figure goes here. Letters (A, B, Y, ZB…) mark subtotals that a later line refers back to.
IndentationIndented lines are workings feeding the subtotal on the right; they do not themselves enter the main column.
Single ruleA subtotal.
Double ruleA figure the question asks for, or one that carries into the next stage.
Three rules that apply to every computation here
  1. Head the computation. Taxpayer name, which tax, and the year of assessment. Three lines, and they are marks.
  2. Show the working, not just the answer. ($30,000 × 12) beside the figure earns the mark even if the arithmetic slips.
  3. Name every subtotal correctly. "Assessable value", "net assessable value", "assessable profit", "net assessable profit", "net chargeable income" are defined terms with different meanings. They are not interchangeable.

LineWhat belongs there
PremiumSpread evenly over the lease term, capped at 36 months (s.5B(4)). A 3-year lease premium received in one year is taxed 12⁄36 in each.
Irrecoverable rentDeducted in the year it is recognised as irrecoverable, not the year the rent accrued (s.7C(1)). Recovery later is assessable in the year of recovery, not related back.
RatesOnly rates paid by the owner. Rates paid by the tenant never enter the computation.
20% statutory allowanceFixed, automatic, and taken after rates. It is not a claim and no receipts are needed — and no more can be claimed however much was actually spent.
The four things that never get deducted Government rent, mortgage interest, actual repairs, and improvements are all non-deductible under property tax. The 20% allowance is the only relief for outgoings. Mortgage interest is exactly why a mortgaged property owner should look at Personal Assessment.
Worked example Textbook Example 7.1 (Mr Gap), p.101 — rate unchanged, so still current

Mr Gap let a Sai Kung house on a three-year lease from 1 January 2022 at $30,000 a month, with a premium of $180,000 payable on 1 January 2022. In the year ended 31 March 2024 he paid Government rent $10,800, mortgage interest $110,000, rates $18,000, and repairs and improvement $20,000.

2. Salaries Tax IRO ss.8–13 — textbook Ch.12

The structural point worth memorising There is no statutory format for a salaries tax computation. But the layout below puts every item affecting rental value before the rental value line — because rental value is 10% of income after allowable outgoings. Updated: the standard rate is two-tiered from 2024/25 The textbook's format shows the standard rate as a flat 15%. From YA 2024/25 the standard rate is two-tiered: 15% on the first HKD 5,000,000 of net income, 16% on the remainder. Under HKD 5,000,000 the book's line still gives the right answer; above it, the book understates. Write line (c) as the two-tier calculation.
LineWhat catches people out
Rental value base (H)10% of income after s.12(1)(a) outgoings — not of gross salary. 4% for a hotel room / shared boarding-type accommodation, 8% for two rooms in a hotel.
Rent suffered (K)Deducted from rental value, and only down to nil — it cannot create a negative.
Donations cap (S)35% of P — income after the outgoings and rental value but before self-education and the other concessionary deductions.
Order of Q and S–ZSelf-education is a s.12 deduction and comes before the concessionary block. Putting it among the concessionary deductions changes the donations cap base.
The standard-rate base (ZB)Net income before personal allowances. Applying it to EE is the single most common error in this computation.
Worked example Built on the textbook's Ch.12 layout, at YA 2026/27 rates

Mr Pak earns salary $900,000 and a bonus $100,000, pays a professional subscription of $2,000, and is provided with a rent-free flat by his employer. He donated $30,000 to an approved charity and made mandatory MPF contributions of $18,000. He is single with no dependants.

3. Profits Tax IRO ss.14–19C — textbook Ch.20

Start from profit per the accounts — never rebuild from gross profit The textbook sets this out as an explicit WRONG vs CORRECT contrast, and it is worth the space. A profits tax computation is an adjustment of the accounting profit, not a fresh income statement.

✗ Wrong — the gross profits method

Gross profitX
Add: assessable incomeX
Less: allowable expenses under the IRO(X)
Less: depreciation allowances(X)
Less: charitable donation(X)
Assessable profitX

Rebuilding the whole profit from scratch. It loses the audit trail back to the accounts, and any expense you forget to re-add is silently allowed.

✓ Correct — adjust the accounting profit

Profit / (loss) per the accountsX
Add: non-allowable expenses chargedX
Less: non-assessable income credited, and deductible amounts not in the accounts(X)

Three ordering traps in this format 1. The charitable donation is added back first and relieved last, because its 35% cap is measured on Y — after depreciation allowances. Deducting it early gives the wrong cap.
2. Depreciation allowances come off before the donation, never after.
3. Only one entity in a group may claim the two-tiered first-tier rate in a year. Where the question mentions connected entities, say which one is nominated.
Worked example Built on the textbook's Ch.20 format, current rates

A Hong Kong company's accounts show a profit of $2,500,000. Charged in arriving at it: depreciation $300,000, a charitable donation $80,000, a fine $20,000, and a general provision for doubtful debts $50,000. Credited to it: dividend income $100,000 and offshore trading profit $200,000. Depreciation allowances for the year are $260,000, and there is a loss brought forward of $310,000. It is the nominated group entity for the two-tiered rate.

4. Depreciation allowances — plant and machinery IRO Part 6 — textbook Ch.31

Plant and machinery is grouped into three pools by annual allowance rate — 10%, 20% and 30%. The schedule below is the standard layout; with it memorised the work becomes filling in blanks.

PointDetail
Initial allowance, 60%On the addition, not on the pool. Taken before the annual allowance, so the annual allowance is computed on the reduced figure.
Order: IA → disposal → AADisposals come out after the initial allowance and before the annual allowance. Reversing this changes every figure below it.
Four kinds of additionCash purchase — pooled, 60% IA on the cash price. Hire purchase — not pooled; 60% IA only on the capital portion of the deposit and instalments paid in the year. Private asset introduced into the business — pooled, but no initial allowance. Asset used partly privately — not pooled; dealt with separately and apportioned.
Balancing adjustmentArises only when the pool is emptied — proceeds exceeding the pool give a balancing charge; a residue with no assets left gives a balancing allowance. A disposal from a continuing pool creates neither.
Prescribed fixed assets100% deduction under s.16G — these never enter the pool at all. They belong in the profits tax computation, not here.

5. Personal Assessment IRO ss.40–43

The one format that consumes the other four: it takes the net assessable value from the property computation, the net assessable income from the salaries computation and the net assessable profit from the profits computation, and re-taxes the lot at progressive rates.

Joint election — apportioning the tax Where a married couple elects jointly, the allowances come off the joint reduced total income and the tax is computed once, then split in the ratio of each spouse's reduced total income — not gross. Each receives their own notice of assessment.

What has changed since the textbook

The 22nd edition states the law to 31 May 2024. Every rate and figure in the formats above was re-checked against IRD. Here is what moved and what did not, so you can see that the unchanged items were checked rather than assumed.

ItemTextbook (to 31 May 2024)Current — used above
Salaries tax standard rate Flat 15% on net income (Ch.12 format, line c) changed Two-tiered from YA 2024/25: 15% on the first $5,000,000, 16% on the remainder
Personal allowances Basic $132,000 / married $264,000 / child $130,000 era changed From YA 2026/27: basic $145,000, married $290,000, child $140,000, single parent $145,000, dependent parent 60+ $55,000 (IRD pam61e)
One-off tax reduction Table runs to 2023/24, ceiling $3,000 changed A 100% reduction capped at $3,000 also applied for 2025/26. Check the current Budget — none is assumed in the examples above
Child allowance, newborns Not in this edition proposed 2026 Policy Address: $160,000 for a 2nd or subsequent child born on/after 16 Sep 2026 — needs an amendment ordinance, so not used above. details →
Property tax standard rate15%unchanged 15%
20% statutory repair allowance20% of AV after ratesunchanged
Two-tiered profits tax rates8.25% / 16.5% and 7.5% / 15%, first $2,000,000unchanged
Salaries tax progressive bands2/6/10/14% on four $50,000 bands, then 17%unchanged
Initial allowance on P&M60%unchanged

Not reflected in the textbook's chapters at all The 22nd edition predates the 2026 stamp duty amendments, the 2026 bills on funds/FIHV/carried interest and on shipping and commodity trading, the CARF and amended-CRS legislation, and the September 2026 Policy Address measures. None of those change the five computation formats above, but they do change the surrounding rules — see IRD What's New.
