IRD Administration

Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) — filing, assessment, objections, appeals, provisional tax and penalty mechanics that apply across Profits, Property and Salaries Tax.

    Source note The section numbers on this page reflect the IRO's long-standing, stable administration framework, but this build's local CAP112 text extraction did not cleanly surface the Part 9–14 body text for direct verification (see §7). Cross-check exact wording against a fresh Cap. 112 download before citing a subsection in a formal position.

    1. Overview

    These provisions are common infrastructure across Profits Tax, Property Tax and Salaries Tax — a computation is only complete once you know how it gets filed, assessed, and (if needed) disputed or paid provisionally.

    2. Returns & Filing IRO s.51

    The Commissioner issues annual returns (profits tax, salaries tax, property tax, employer's return) which must be completed and filed by the stated due date — extensions are routinely available for corporate profits tax returns filed through a tax representative under the Block Extension Scheme. Every person carrying on a trade, profession or business must keep sufficient records (s.51C) for at least 7 years to enable profits/losses to be readily ascertained.

    Practical note For a newly incorporated company, the first profits tax return is usually issued around 18 months after incorporation or commencement of business — but a company must still notify the Commissioner of chargeability (s.51(2)) within 4 months after the end of the basis period if no return has been issued, even where no return has yet been received.
    Block Extension Scheme — 2025/26 returns, "D" code extended By circular letter of 14 July 2026, the Commissioner further extended the due date for 2025/26 profits tax returns with Accounting Date Code "D" (accounting date falling between 1 and 31 December 2025):
    • Paper filing: 17 August 2026 → 31 August 2026
    • Electronic filing: 17 September 2026 → 2 October 2026
    The extension was granted to help businesses and practitioners adapt to new compliance requirements; the circular still urges representatives to file well before the extended date. Codes "N" and "M" were not changed by this circular. Source: Circular Letter to Tax Representatives — Block Extension Scheme for Lodgement of 2025/26 Tax Returns, 14 Jul 2026.
    Business Tax Portal — new Responsible Person capacities (8 Jun 2026) IRD expanded the capacities in which a Responsible Person may register a business for the Business Tax Portal (BTP). Check the registration capacity before opening a BTP account for a client entity — an account opened in the wrong capacity has to be redone.

    3. Assessment & Estimated Assessment IRO ss.59–60

    The assessor assesses the return as filed, or — where no return is filed, or the assessor is not satisfied with a return — issues an estimated assessment based on the best information available. Estimated assessments are commonly issued precisely to protect the statutory time limit for assessment where a return is outstanding; they are not a final determination and are superseded once the actual figures are agreed (usually via an objection, §4).

    Additional assessments may be raised within the statutory time limit (generally 6 years from the end of the relevant year of assessment, extended to 10 years in cases involving fraud or wilful default) where profits/income have been understated or under-assessed.

    4. Objections & Appeals IRO s.64 (objections), s.66 (Board of Review), s.69 (case stated)

    StageTime limitForum
    Notice of objection1 month from the date of the assessmentCommissioner of Inland Revenue
    Appeal against the Commissioner's determination1 month from the determinationBoard of Review
    Case stated appeal on a point of law1 month from the Board's decisionCourt of First Instance, then Court of Appeal / Court of Final Appeal

    An objection must state precisely the grounds of objection — a bare, unparticularised objection can be rejected as invalid. Tax under dispute is not automatically suspended; the taxpayer must separately apply to hold over payment of the tax in dispute (or the portion in dispute) pending resolution.

    5. Provisional Tax & Holdover IRO ss.63A–63U, 71

    Provisional profits tax, provisional salaries tax, and provisional property tax are each charged in advance, generally based on the immediately preceding year's assessable profits/income, and later set off against the final assessment for that year once it issues. A taxpayer may apply to hold over payment of the whole or part of provisional tax where, for example, the current year's profits/income are objectively likely to be significantly lower than the prior year's, or personal allowances have increased, or an objection is pending on the immediately preceding year's assessment.

    Practical note Holdover applications have their own strict statutory deadlines (broadly, the later of 28 days before the provisional tax is due, or 14 days after the provisional tax notice is issued) — missing the window forfeits the holdover regardless of the merits.

    6. Late Payment of Tax — Surcharge and Recovery IRO s.71; Part 12

    The consequence almost every taxpayer actually meets, and the one most often confused with the penalties in §7. A surcharge under s.71 is not a penalty for an offence — it is automatic, needs no intent, no reasonable-excuse enquiry and no hearing. Miss the date and it is imposed.

    WhenWhat happensProvision
    The due date passes A 5% surcharge is imposed on the tax unpaid. Recovery action may begin immediately and without prior notice. s.71(5)
    6 months after the due date A further 10% surcharge on the amount still unpaid — including the 5% already imposed. s.71(5A)
    The 10% is charged on the tax plus the first surcharge — so it is not 15% On tax of $100,000 left unpaid for over six months:
    Tax due100,000
    Add: 5% surcharge — s.71(5)5,000
    Amount unpaid after the due date105,000
    Add: further 10% on $105,000 — s.71(5A)10,500
    Total payable115,500
    Total surcharge $15,500 — 15.5% of the original tax, not 15%. The second surcharge compounds on the first.

    The instalment trap

    Where a demand note is split into two instalments, missing the first makes the second immediately due, and the 5% is then charged on the whole outstanding balance — not just on the instalment that was missed.

    Demand note of $100,000: first instalment $70,000, second $30,000
    First instalment paid on time, second missed — 5% × $30,0001,500
    First instalment missed — the second accelerates, so 5% × $100,0005,000
    Cost of missing the earlier date rather than the later one3,500

    Left unpaid for six months, that same note reaches $115,500.

    Recovery of tax in default IRO Part 12

    IRD does not wait for the six-month mark before acting. Its own notice states that other than imposing the 5% surcharge it "will also immediately commence other recovery actions without prior notice":

    ActionWhat it means in practice
    Recovery notice to a third party
    s.76    		 Served on anyone who owes or holds money for the defaulter — employer, bank, tenant, debtor, customer. That person must pay the money to IRD instead, and is discharged to the extent they do. This is why a tax default can become visible to an employer or banker.
    Civil proceedings
    s.75    		 Tax in default is recoverable as a civil debt in the District Court. A judgment carries the usual consequences, including costs and interest on the judgment debt.
    Departure prevention order
    s.77    		 The Commissioner may apply to a District Judge for a direction stopping a defaulter from leaving Hong Kong until the tax is paid or security given.
    Joint and several liability
    property tax co-owners    		 Co-owners are liable jointly and severally: IRD may pursue any one of them for the whole amount, not merely their share. Once one pays in full, recovery against the others stops. Tax is never collected twice, and any overpayment is refunded.
    Applying to pay by instalments does not stop the surcharge A taxpayer in genuine financial difficulty may apply to settle by instalments. But IRD is explicit that even where the application is approved, surcharges are still imposed on tax remaining unpaid after the due date. An instalment arrangement buys time and heads off recovery action; it is not a waiver. Conditional waiver schemes have been offered in particular years as a relief measure, but they are exceptional and year-specific — never assume one applies.
    Keep the two regimes apart — this is the distinction to have straight
    Surcharge, s.71Penalty, ss.80 / 82A / 82
    Triggered byPaying lateFailing to file or notify, filing an incorrect return, or evading tax
    Fault requiredNone — automatic on the dateAbsence of reasonable excuse; for s.82, wilful intent
    Amount5%, then a further 10% on the totalUp to treble the tax undercharged, plus a fine and, under s.82, imprisonment
    Who imposes itThe Commissioner, administrativelyThe Commissioner (s.82A) or a court (ss.80, 82)
    A taxpayer who files a correct return on time but pays late faces the surcharge only. One who files late and pays late faces both.

    Source: IRD, Pay Tax On Time to Avoid Penalty and Recovery Actions, and GovHK Consequences of Not Paying Tax On Time and Recovering Tax in Default.

    7. Penalties IRO ss.80, 82, 82A

    ContraventionConsequence
    Failure to file a return, or filing late, without reasonable excuseSummary conviction fine and/or additional tax (s.80)
    Making an incorrect return, or omitting income, without reasonable excuseAdditional tax up to treble the tax undercharged (s.82A) — an administrative penalty assessed by the Commissioner, distinct from criminal prosecution
    Wilfully with intent to evade, or assisting another to evade, taxCriminal prosecution (s.82) — fine up to treble the tax undercharged plus imprisonment up to 3 years

    Additional tax under s.82A is an administrative sanction (no need to prove criminal intent) and can be settled by way of a compound penalty offer from the Commissioner in less serious cases — this is the mechanism most commonly encountered in routine tax computation corrections and voluntary disclosures.

    8. Double Taxation Relief & Tax Clearance IRO ss.49, 50, 51D

    Where Hong Kong has a comprehensive double taxation agreement (CDTA) with another jurisdiction, s.50 provides a tax credit for foreign tax paid on income also taxed in Hong Kong (interacting with the s.16(1)(c)/(2J) deduction restriction on the Profits Tax page). Employers must comply with tax clearance obligations (s.51D) before an employee who is about to leave Hong Kong permanently, or for a substantial period, ceases employment — including withholding final payments until IRD confirms clearance or a specified period has elapsed.

    CDTA network (as at 24 September 2026): Hong Kong signed its 60th comprehensive double taxation agreement with Slovenia on 4 September 2026, following Nigeria (13 July 2026) and Cyprus (12 June 2026). Each enters into force only after both sides complete ratification — check the IRD CDTA list for the in-force date before claiming treaty relief.

    9. Automatic Exchange of Information & Crypto-Asset Reporting IRO Part 8A

    Two separate 2026 measures extend Hong Kong's information-exchange obligations, and both change what a reporting financial institution has to collect:

    • Amended CRS — enacted. The Inland Revenue (Amendment) (Automatic Exchange of Information) Bill 2026 passed on 17 June 2026, implementing the OECD's amended Common Reporting Standard.
    • CARF — bill stage. The Inland Revenue (Amendment) (Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework and Amended Common Reporting Standard) Bill 2026 was introduced into LegCo on 1 June 2026, and IRD published its Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework page on 22 June 2026. CARF brings crypto-asset service providers into the reporting net — a genuinely new population of reporting entities, not just a new field on an existing return.
    Policy watch The Advisory Committee on Tax Policy held its first meeting on 29 June 2026 — the standing forum now shaping medium-term HK tax policy. IRD also responded to an Ombudsman direct investigation report on 31 August 2026, and published its Annual Report on Performance Pledge 2025-26 on 9 July 2026 (useful for expected turnaround times on objections and refunds).
    Fraud alerts — tell clients IRD issued fraudulent-email alerts on 6 July, 22 July, 4 September and 15 September 2026 (fake prosecution notices, fake tax audit notices, and phishing for email passwords), plus a 25 August 2026 warning not to download the eTAX Mobile Application from unofficial channels. IRD does not request passwords or payment by email link.
