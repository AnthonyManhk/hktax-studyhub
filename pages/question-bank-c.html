Question 1 — Mr Ivan Ho (15 marks)

Scenario15 marks

Ivan Ho is employed on an annual salary of $1,020,000 for the year of assessment 2026/27. During the year he incurred $60,000 on a job-related course, qualifying as a deductible self-education expense, and made the mandatory MPF contribution of $18,000. He is married; his wife has no income of her own.

Ivan also wholly owns a separate residential flat, which he lets out unconnected with his employment. Its net assessable value for the year (after rates and the 20% statutory allowance) is $200,000. He pays mortgage interest of $150,000 a year on the loan used to acquire that flat.

(a) Compute Ivan's salaries tax liability for the year, ignoring any election for personal assessment. (10 marks)

(b) Advise, with supporting calculations, whether Ivan should elect personal assessment for the year. (5 marks)

Question 2 — XYZ Trading Ltd (25 marks)

Scenario25 marks

XYZ Trading Ltd, a Hong Kong company with no connected entities, reports a net profit per accounts of $8,000,000 for the year ended 31 March 2027. The accounts include depreciation of $450,000, approved charitable donations of $200,000, and legal fees of $60,000 for acquiring a new five-year retail lease.

The company's plant and machinery pool (20% pool) had a written-down value brought forward of $600,000; during the year it bought qualifying plant for $800,000 and sold plant for $100,000. Separately, it incurred $1,500,000 of qualifying Type B R&D expenditure, already charged as an expense (and so already included) in the $8,000,000 net profit above. The company also has an unrelieved trading loss of $500,000 brought forward from an earlier year.

(a) Compute XYZ Trading Ltd's profits tax payable for the year. (20 marks)

(b) XYZ Trading Ltd is considering whether the $500,000 loss could instead be surrendered to a profitable fellow group company. Advise, with reasons. (5 marks)

Total for part (a): 20 marks. Stating the total enhanced R&D deduction ($4,500,000) instead of the additional amount still to be deducted ($3,000,000), and computing the annual allowance on the pool before deducting the initial allowance, are the two errors this question is designed to catch.

(b) (5 marks): Hong Kong has no group loss relief of any kind. Each company, including XYZ Trading Ltd and any fellow group company, is a separate person chargeable to profits tax in its own right (2 marks). The $500,000 loss cannot be surrendered to, or set off against, a fellow group company's profits, however profitable that company is or however closely the two are connected (2 marks). The loss simply remains within XYZ Trading Ltd itself, carried forward indefinitely with no time limit, to be set off only against XYZ's own future assessable profits from the same trade (1 mark).

Question 3 — Mr Anthony Cheung (15 marks)

Scenario15 marks

Anthony Cheung owns two properties in Hong Kong, both let in his own name during the year of assessment 2026/27.

The shop was let for the whole year at $45,000 a month. Anthony paid rates of $20,000 on it. The tenant fell into difficulty and three months' rent was proved irrecoverable during the year.

The flat was let for the whole year at $22,000 a month, and Anthony also received a premium of $540,000 on granting that five-year lease at the start of the year. The flat's tenant pays the rates direct to the Government.

(a) Compute Anthony's property tax liability for the year. (11 marks)

(b) Anthony is considering transferring both properties into a Hong Kong company he owns, which carries on a trading business here. Explain the property tax consequences. (4 marks)

Total for part (a): 11 marks. Each property is computed separately, with its own rates deduction and its own 20% allowance — aggregating the two into one computation loses marks. Spreading the premium over the full 60 months (giving $108,000) instead of applying the 36-month cap is the other error this question tests.

(b) (4 marks): A corporation carrying on a trade or business in Hong Kong may apply under s.5(2)(a) for exemption from property tax, on the basis that the rental income is instead brought into its profits tax computation (2 marks). If no such application is made, property tax is charged on the company in the ordinary way, but s.25 then sets that property tax off against the profits tax payable, so the same rent is not taxed twice either way (1 mark). Note the rate difference this creates: the rental profit would move from the flat 15% property tax charge to the corporate two-tier profits tax rates of 8.25%/16.5%, and the company could then deduct actual outgoings such as mortgage interest and repairs rather than being confined to the fixed 20% statutory allowance (1 mark).

Question 4 — Summit & Vale Partners (25 marks)

Scenario25 marks

Summit & Vale Partners is a Hong Kong partnership with two partners: Mr Vale, an individual, holding a 70% profit share, and Summit Holdings Ltd, a Hong Kong company, holding the remaining 30%.

The partnership's accounts for the year ended 31 March 2027 show a net profit of $6,400,000, after charging: a partner's salary of $900,000 paid to Mr Vale, a salary of $300,000 paid to Mr Vale's spouse (who works in the business), depreciation of $350,000, and approved charitable donations of $120,000.

The partnership's 30% plant and machinery pool had a written-down value brought forward of $500,000. During the year it bought qualifying plant for $900,000 and sold plant for $400,000.

(a) Compute the partnership's assessable profit for the year. (14 marks)

(b) Compute the profits tax payable by each partner, and in total. (8 marks)

(c) Mr Vale asks whether Summit Holdings Ltd could elect personal assessment on its share. Advise. (3 marks)

Total for part (a): 14 marks. Both the partner's own salary and the salary to his spouse are added back under s.17(2) — a partnership cannot deduct either, and forgetting the spouse's salary is the commonest omission. Computing the annual allowance on the pool before deducting the initial allowance is the other error tested here.

Total for part (b): 8 marks. The $2,000,000 two-tier band is apportioned between the partners in the profit-sharing ratio, and each partner's slice is then taxed at their own rates — 7.5%/15% for the individual, 8.25%/16.5% for the corporate partner. Applying one flat set of rates to the whole $7,272,000, as if the partnership were a single taxpayer, is the error a mixed partnership is set to catch.

(c) (3 marks): No. Personal assessment is available to individuals only (1 mark). Summit Holdings Ltd is a company, so its share of the partnership's assessable profit simply stays in its own profits tax computation at the corporate rates, with no personal assessment route available to it (1 mark). Mr Vale, being an individual, could elect on his own share — but on these figures it would not help him: his share alone far exceeds the level at which the 17% marginal progressive rate overtakes the 15% standard rate, and there is no loss or interest here for the election to unlock (1 mark).
